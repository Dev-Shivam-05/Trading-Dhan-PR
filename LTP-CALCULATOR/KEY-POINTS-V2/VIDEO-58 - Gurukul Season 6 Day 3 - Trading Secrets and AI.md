## Expiry-day mechanics covered in passing

- Implied volatility must reach zero by expiry. Once it does, the time value of every option collapses with it, and the Greeks follow.
- The reason given: implied volatility reflects the gap between the spot and the futures price — on the day shown, around 15 points — and on expiry day that gap is forced to close, so spot and futures must converge.
- A rule change introduced earlier in the year roughly doubles margin on expiry day. To avoid an automatic square-off, keep double the margin in the account a day in advance, or close positions the day before.

## What the video tells you to do

- Before placing a trade, measure the stop-loss distance from your entry level to the next level and reject the trade if the risk-reward does not justify it.
- When both sides carry the same 100-point risk, take no directional bias and trade whichever side sets up first.
- Argue against your own trade first — look for reasons not to take it — rather than looking for confirmation.
- Once the lines tell you to exit, exit. Do not average down or hold waiting for the scenario to flip back.
- If you hold positions into expiry day, fund double margin the day before or square off in advance.
