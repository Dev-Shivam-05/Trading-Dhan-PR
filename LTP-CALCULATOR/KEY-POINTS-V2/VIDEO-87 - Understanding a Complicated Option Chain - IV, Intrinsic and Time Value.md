## Why the option does not follow the stock

- The setup discussed is a trader hoping the underlying climbs to a target and expecting the option to follow it point for point. The speaker says that will not happen.
- At the level discussed, the option is trading near ₹55 and the trader is counting on roughly a ₹40 move in the underlying.
- Because delta is around half, a ₹40 move in the underlying translates into only about ₹20 on the option.
- That gain is not free. While the underlying takes time to travel, time value is decaying every minute, so part of the ₹20 is given back before it is realised.
- The move also tends to fall short of the hoped-for level — the example is a target of 150 where only 170 is reached — so the option does not return to its earlier price.
- Net result in the example: the option comes back to roughly ₹35 to ₹45 instead of the full ₹55, and the trader ends up capturing only 7 to 10 points.

## What the video tells you to do

- Before buying an option on a directional view, work out what the expected move is worth after delta, rather than assuming the option moves with the underlying.
- Subtract the time value that will decay over the period you expect to hold, because the wait itself costs money.
