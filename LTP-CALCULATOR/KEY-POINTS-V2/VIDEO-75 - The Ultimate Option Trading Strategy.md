## The two numbers being used

- **Open interest** is the writer's position that is still open — sold and not yet bought back.
- **Volume** counts every lot traded during the day, and multiplies by lot size. A trade creates two volumes, one for the buy and one for the sell.

## Finding the real at-the-money strike

- The spot price is drawn on the chain as a red imaginary line. In the session it sits at about 25,046, between the 25,000 and 25,050 strikes.
- Which of those two is at the money is decided by **time value, not by distance**. Open the **IVTV** tool at the top of the chain to show implied volatility, intrinsic value and time value per strike.
- The strike carrying the highest time value is the at-the-money strike. Here the call side shows ₹66 and the put side ₹63 of time value at 25,050, making 25,050 the ATM.
- Every reading of support and resistance starts from that strike.

## Reading support and resistance off the chain

- From the imaginary line, move outward on the call side starting from the lower of the two strikes. The nearest strike holding the largest open interest or largest volume is **resistance**.
- On the put side, start from the higher strike and move outward into out-of-the-money territory. The nearest strike holding the largest open interest or volume is **support**.

## The pressure markers

- **WTB** — weak towards bottom. Normally bearish pressure.
- **WTT** — weak towards top. Normally bullish pressure.
- **SOC** — state of confusion. It appears when support and resistance stop giving a clear reading. The marker is coloured red when the confusion sits on the support side.
- SOC is triggered by the 75% threshold. When the second open interest or volume passes 75% of the leader, the tool warns that it could take over at any moment. Below 74.99% the warning clears.

## What the video tells you to do

- Identify the ATM by highest time value using IVTV before reading anything else off the chain.
- Walk outward from the imaginary line to find the nearest large open interest or volume on each side — that is your support and resistance for the day.
- Treat an SOC warning as a signal that the level may be about to change, not as a trade signal in itself.
