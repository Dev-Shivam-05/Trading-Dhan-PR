## What volume is and where it comes from

- Volume is created by a completed trade: a buyer and a seller matching. Buy five SBI shares while someone sells five, and five units of volume exist.
- Every executed trade sets a new Last Traded Price, so volume and LTP are inseparable. Volume cannot change without the price changing.
- Volume is an intraday number. It starts at zero at 9:15 am, accumulates until 3:30 pm and resets the next morning.
- This is option-chain volume at each strike, not the volume bars under a price chart.

## Where the volume actually sits

- Volume builds at many strikes at once, far beyond the few near the money that most traders watch. The video points to activity at strikes several hundred points away in both directions.
- Out-of-the-money strikes carry heavy volume; in-the-money strikes carry much less. Out-of-the-money is where the writing happens.
- Much of that writing is institutional and algorithmic. Those desks are not chasing overnight returns; they run a fixed process for a modest annual number and are indifferent to intraday moves.
- An intraday trader therefore cares about where the crowd is trading today, not about which strike expires worthless — which makes the heaviest volume the relevant number.

## Why the second highest volume is the guide

- On the LTP Calculator the largest volume on each side is highlighted in blue and treated as 100%. Every other strike is expressed as a percentage of it.
- The second highest volume is highlighted separately in yellow, and only when it is actually guiding. If it is neither bold nor highlighted, the speaker says it is giving no signal.
- The claim: the highest volume holds the market at its strike; the second highest tells you which way price wants to travel from there.
- The test is whether the second highest is gaining on the highest. If its percentage keeps climbing towards 100%, price moves towards that strike. If it stalls, it does not.
- When the second highest finally reaches 100% it becomes the highest, the yellow highlight jumps to a new strike, and the reading starts again from that new second highest.

## What the video tells you to do

- Read the second highest volume on each side, not the highest, and watch whether its percentage is rising or falling.
- Treat a rising second-highest percentage as pressure towards that strike, and a stalled one as a pause in the move.
- Check both sides. A move extends only when the call side and the put side are guiding the same way.
- Ignore a second highest that is neither bold nor highlighted — on this tool that means it is not guiding.
