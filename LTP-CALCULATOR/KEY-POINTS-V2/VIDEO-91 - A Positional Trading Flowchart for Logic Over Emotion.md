## The bullish setup

- Shifting status must be **Strong** or **WTT**.
- The put-side HOI reversal must show a breakdown, or sit at the current market price.
- Star rating must not be zero, and not more than one.
- If all three conditions hold, buy at the current market price.
- **Target:** the strike price at the call-side HOI reversal / the 100% open interest level.
- **Stop loss:** if the put side's 100% open interest level turns WTB (Weak Towards Bottom), exit.
- **Hedge:** subtract the put HOI reversal from the call HOI reversal, and buy a put whose premium is about 5-7% of that difference, for the same quantity as your shares or futures lots. On a 1,000-share position the example premium is roughly ₹10 to ₹17, and the premium paid is effectively the cost of the stop.

## The bearish setup

- Same structure, mirrored. Shifting status must be Strong or WT.
- The call-side HOI reversal must break down at or near the current market price.
- Star rating again not zero and not more than one.
- Sell from the current market price. **Target:** the strike price at the put HOI reversal's 100% open interest level. **Stop loss:** when the call side's 100% level turns WT.
- **Hedge:** subtract the call HOI reversal from the put HOI reversal and buy a call at about 5-7% of that value, held alongside the futures position.

## What the video tells you to do

- Trade this setup in cash and futures only. The guide states explicitly that no naked option buying is to be done on it.
- Check all three conditions before entering; the chart is a gate, not a suggestion.
- Buy the protective option at the same quantity as the underlying position, so the premium becomes a fixed, known risk.
- Read the written guide alongside the chart, since every abbreviation on it — call HOI reversal, put HOI reversal, shifting status, WT, WTB, Strong, star rating, CMP — is defined there.
