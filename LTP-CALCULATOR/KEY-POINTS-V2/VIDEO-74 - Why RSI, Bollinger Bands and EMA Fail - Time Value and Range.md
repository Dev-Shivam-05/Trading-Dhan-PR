## The idea behind the lines

- The bands come from standard deviation, the statistical rule that a deviating quantity falls inside successive ranges with rising probability.
- The speaker gives rough figures: the first range holds roughly 65% of the time, the second roughly 95%, the third roughly 99%. He states plainly that these are approximate and that this is textbook mathematics, not his invention.
- Three lines above price are resistance, three below are support, labelled L1, L2 and L3 outward from price. RL1, RL2 and RL3 appear on the resistance side.
- The calculation is done from the option chain and the Greeks, not from historical price data.

## Weekly range and monthly range

- **Weekly range** applies to instruments with weekly expiry, so Nifty. Click the **W** button next to the spot lines on the chart.
- New weekly lines are generated after the market closes on Thursday, published around 4:00 pm, and are on the chart when Friday opens. They do not move during the week.
- **Monthly range** applies to instruments with monthly expiry — Bank Nifty, Mid Cap Nifty, Fin Nifty and individual stocks. Click **M**.
- Monthly lines appear the day after the monthly expiry. If August expires on the 28th, the September lines are visible from the 29th, so a position can be planned from day one of the period.

## What the video tells you to do

- **Option writing.** Sell the L1 strikes on both sides for the most aggressive position, L2 on both sides for a safer one, L3 on both sides for the safest. Do this at the start of the expiry period so the full time value is available to you.
- Match the range to the expiry cycle — weekly range for Nifty, monthly range for Bank Nifty, the other indices and stocks.
- **Cash buying.** Buy a stock when it reaches monthly L1 if you accept risk, L2 if you want more margin of safety.
- Study the historical data free of charge and verify the behaviour yourself before trading it.
