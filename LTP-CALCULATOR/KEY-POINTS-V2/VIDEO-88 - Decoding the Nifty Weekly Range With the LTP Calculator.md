## Where the range lines come from

- At the start of a week or month, writers build large open interest and volume at particular strikes because each of them is fixing a range they expect price to stay inside.
- That range depends on the gap between spot and futures prices and on the Greeks it produces — delta, theta, vega, gamma, rho and the minor ones. The speaker treats Greek positioning as a readable record of what participants are doing at each strike.
- Reading this by hand is the problem: open interest and volumes shift between strikes all day, so following the shifts means watching the market continuously.
- The tool's stated answer is that its system tracks the chain through the session and again around 4:00 pm as the market settles, then computes the range from that.
- The weekly range is a **static** line. It is calculated after Tuesday's expiry at 4:00 pm and then fixed for the week — it does not move as price moves.
- The lines are derived from the option chain, not from historical price data. The speaker repeats this point on every stock he shows.
