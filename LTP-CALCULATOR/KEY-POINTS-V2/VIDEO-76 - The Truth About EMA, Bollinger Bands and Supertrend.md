## In one line

The full rules of the 9:20 strategy — four price lines that are fixed for the day at 9:21 am — set side by side with a 9 EMA to argue that a line known in advance beats one that only forms after the move.

## What the 9:20 lines are

- Click the **920** button on the right of the chart at or after 9:21 am. Four lines appear.
- They are static. Once set at 9:21 they do not change for the rest of the session, and they stay the same on historical dates.
- In the session shown, recorded on 28 August with the market near 24,652 at 11 am, the four lines were: **UR+1 at 24,668**, **EOR at 24,620**, **EOS at 24,575**, **US-1 at 24,529**.
- EOR is the extension of resistance line, EOS the extension of support line. The theory attached to them: once the market has reversed from these lines and completed its targets, that range holds for the day.
- If all four lines are not present, there is no trade. If a line is missing on one side, there is no trade on that side.

## The writing strategy

- At 9:21, with all four lines present, sell a call at a strike above UR+1 and sell a put at a strike below US-1. In the session that meant selling the 24,700 call at about ₹29 and the 24,500 put at about ₹8.
- The stop loss is the total premium collected on both legs — about ₹37–38 here.
- If one side hits that stop loss, square off that leg. The other leg is expected to decay to zero, so the day ends around no profit and no loss.
- If neither side is hit, exit both legs at 2:30 pm. On expiry day exit at 3:30 pm instead.

## The buying strategy

- **Bullish, aggressive:** buy a Call when the market reaches EOS. Then click **Set D** to generate the target, choosing D1, D2 or D3.
- If the market falls further to US-1, average with a double lot — one lot at EOS, two at US-1 — and click Set D again. The put-side D value becomes the stop loss and the upper D value the target.
- **Bullish, moderate:** skip EOS entirely and buy only at US-1, then set target and stop loss from Set D and exit there.
- **Bearish:** buy a Put at EOR or UR. If the target comes, exit. If instead the market rises to UR+1, double-lot average there; a moderate trader takes a fresh put trade at UR+1 rather than averaging.
- The full rule set is drawn as a flow chart, reachable from the question mark button in the tool.

## The comparison with EMA

- The EMA used is a 9 EMA on a 5-minute chart, the common intraday setting, plotted in TradingView.
- At the morning reversal the EMA line had not formed yet, so it gave no buy signal at the low. The 9:20 EOS line was already on the chart from 9:21.
- The speaker's core complaint is that the EMA keeps repeating buy after the move is under way, and only turns to sell once price has already fallen through it — so a trader is stopped out on one side and then caught on the other.
