Day one of a seven-day public experiment in which a complete beginner is taught, live, why call premiums rise when the index rises and put premiums fall — and why a buyer takes an option instead of a futures contract.

## Lesson one: which side to buy

- The day's chart shows two moves. On the fall, money is made by selling first; on the rise, by buying first. Profit in either direction comes from buying lower and selling higher — only the order changes.
- If you expect the index to rise, buy a call. If you expect it to fall, buy a put.
- Reading the option chain: with the index around 25,540 and the 25,500 strike in view, the call premium on the left was about ₹63 and the put premium on the right about ₹28.
- The student is made to watch a single strike for several minutes until the index has moved 10 to 15 points, and to confirm for himself that the call side grew while the put side shrank. The instruction is to compare one strike against itself, not to look across at other strikes.

## Why a beginner buys the option and not the future

- The futures price was 25,636 and the lot size is 75, so the full contract represents about ₹19.22 lakh.
- The exchange does not demand the full amount — roughly ₹1.25 lakh of margin buys the futures position, about a tenth of its value.
- Someone without that margin buys a slice of the same move instead: an option premium. At ₹62 a unit that is about ₹4,650 for the lot, and profit or loss is scaled to that ₹4,650.
- Analogy used: instead of writing all eight exam papers yourself, eight people each write one. You only earn the marks of the paper you wrote — a smaller stake, a smaller result.
- Cheaper, further-out premiums move less per 100-point index move; more expensive, nearer premiums move more. The choice of strike decides how much of the index move you capture.

## Delta, in its simplest form

- With all other Greeks switched off, delta on the two sides of a strike is shown to sum to about 1.
- In the example, the call side carried about 0.60 and the put side about 0.40. So for a ₹1 rise in the index, the call premium gains roughly 60 paise while the put loses roughly 40 paise, and the reverse on a fall.
- This is presented as the answer to "how much does it move" once the student has understood "which way does it move".

## What the video tells you to do

- Pick one strike price and watch its call and put premiums side by side against a live index for several minutes before trading anything.
- Write down a baseline — the index level and both premiums — and wait for a ₹10 to ₹15 change in the premium before drawing conclusions.
- Buy a call when you expect a rise and a put when you expect a fall; do not trade a strike you have not watched.
- Check the delta column to judge how much of the index move a given strike will pass on.
