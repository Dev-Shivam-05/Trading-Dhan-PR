Day three of the series: what Open Interest actually counts as opposed to volume, why market orders cost you money, and the speaker's definitions of the imaginary line, support and resistance on the option chain.

## Open Interest is only the trade that happened

- Volume is created the moment a trade executes. Open Interest is created when a writer's sold position is executed and stays open.
- The worked example: you bid for one lot at a ₹76 premium, the speaker offers five lots, one lot matches. The Open Interest is one, not five and not six. Only the executed part counts.
- His analogy for the rest: sitting in a room saying you will buy the Burj Khalifa, or contest and win an election against the Prime Minister, changes nothing in the world. Bid and ask quantities are talk; only the trade is real.
- So a large bid or ask quantity on screen means nothing on its own. If no LTP and no volume have printed at that price, nothing has traded there.

## Expiry, restated for the student

- Cash never expires.
- Futures expire on the last Tuesday of the month, and the position must be squared off by then.
- Options expire on the Tuesday of each week for Nifty; for Bank Nifty, Fin Nifty, Midcap Nifty and stocks, on the last Tuesday of the month. Nifty's weekly expiry used to be Thursday and has moved to Tuesday.

## Market price versus limit price

- A market order fills at whatever the other side is asking, not at the LTP you were looking at.
- Nifty example: LTP around ₹39.85 but the market order fills at ₹40.70 — about a rupee worse, because that was the standing ask.
- A stock example is worse: a put with an LTP near ₹14.70 fills at ₹16.55 — nearly ₹2 above, in an option where ₹2 was the whole target.
- The comparison used is walking into a vegetable market and paying whatever the seller names, or handing over a blank cheque.
- The rule given: enter, target and stop loss all go in at limit price, always.

## What the speaker says a standard option chain is missing

- No Greeks, so delta, gamma, implied volatility, intrinsic value and time value are not visible together with the strikes.
- Finding the highest volume, the second highest, the highest Open Interest or the highest change in Open Interest means scanning long columns by eye, which is slow enough to matter intraday.
- The spot price is not marked against the strikes at all, so you cannot see where the market actually sits.
- On his own tool these are colour-coded — largest volume in blue, largest Open Interest per side in its own colour, second highest marked, each strike expressed as a percentage of the 100% strike — and the day's resistance and support are printed at the top of the screen.

## The imaginary line, support and resistance

- The imaginary line is the speaker's own term, not market terminology: it is the spot price drawn between the two strikes it currently sits between.
- Resistance: from the lower strike of that pair, moving out of the money on the call side, the nearest strike holding the largest Open Interest or the largest volume.
- Support: from the higher strike of that pair, moving outwards, the nearest strike holding the largest Open Interest or largest volume.
- Once you find the level, look at which column reads 100% there. That tells you whether today's resistance or support is made of volume, of Open Interest, or of both.
- His reasoning for why levels exist at all: the market is never motiveless. Like a person leaving home for the shop and returning, price has a destination in each direction before it turns. Extensions of support and resistance are the days it goes further than usual.

## What the video tells you to do

- Judge a strike by its printed volume, LTP and Open Interest, never by bid and ask quantity.
- Place every entry, target and stop loss as a limit order.
- Locate the imaginary line first, then take the nearest large Open Interest or volume outwards on each side as resistance and support.
- Check which column reads 100% at those strikes so you know what the level is built from.
- Square off futures by the last Tuesday of the month and weekly options by Tuesday.
