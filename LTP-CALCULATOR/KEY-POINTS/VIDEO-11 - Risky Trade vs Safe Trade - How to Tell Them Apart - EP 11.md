## In one line

When support and resistance sit at different strike prices, the space between them fills with intermediate levels called diversions, and this episode shows how to count them, plot them, and label each resulting trade as safe or risky.

## The rule for counting diversions

- The previous episode covered support and resistance at the same strike. Here they are at different strikes, which is where diversions appear.
- The count is: number of strike prices lying between support and resistance, plus one.
- Case one - support 19,700, resistance 19,800, with 19,750 in between. One strike in between, so two diversions form.
- Case two - adjacent strikes with nothing in between, so the count starts from zero.
- Case three - support and resistance on the same strike. The speaker counts this as minus one, which cancels the plus one and gives zero diversions. That is why on such a day price runs straight from the extension of support to the extension of resistance with nothing in between.

## Reading the day's four lines

- Example used is 24 July, seen at around 9:19 in the morning: resistance strong at 19,800, support strong at 19,700.
- Do not take the values at the opening bell. Wait until roughly 9:25-9:30 so volume builds and the Greeks settle, then read the extension values.
- On that day the four levels came out as: extension of resistance near 817, upper diversion 767, lower diversion 718, extension of support near 668.
- The diversion values are read by clicking the volume on the in-between strike, 19,750, which gives both the call-side and put-side diversion. Draw all four as horizontal lines before the session develops; price spends the day travelling between them.

## Which of the four lines is safe and which is risky

- The two upper lines are put-buying levels. The top line, the extension of resistance, is the safe put. The diversion just below it is the risky put.
- The two lower lines are call-buying levels. The bottom line, the extension of support, is the safe call. The diversion just above it is the risky call.

## What the video tells you to do

- Take profit each time price reaches a diversion rather than holding through it.
- If a risky trade goes against you, add a second lot only when price reaches the next full value - the safe line. Do not add lots at prices in between; the speaker calls that overtrading and a route to losses.
- If the underlying support-resistance picture changes while you are in a risky trade, do not average. Book the loss, because the entry was the risky one.
- Trade this framework roughly between 9:30 and 2:30 and close positions by 2:30.
- After 2:30 stop relying on the option chain. Square-offs and fresh positions distort it, the way a green traffic signal stops meaning much when a school empties onto the road.
