## In one line

How to score a possible intraday trade by listing its strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats in your head before entering, using two live Nifty setups as the worked example.

## The idea being taught

- Every trade signal should be run through a quick four-part check: strength, weakness, opportunity, threat.
- This is a mental exercise, not a written worksheet. It should take seconds and should run automatically the moment a setup appears.
- The point is comparison. When two setups are live at once, the SWOT tells you which one to take and which to leave.
- The scenario changes during the day. Each time the market state changes — blood bath, both-sides-risky, bull run — the old SWOT is void and must be redone.

## The first setup: the S Moderate buy, and why it was dropped

- Market opened with resistance at 25,300 and support strengthening. The state moved from blood bath to both-sides-risky to bull run as the weakness on the support side disappeared.
- Strengths listed: Nifty in a bull run, price hitting the level for the first time, no weakness visible below.
- Weaknesses and threats: the stop loss was around 100 points against a target of only 50; open interest at the 25,500 resistance had climbed to about 74%, which can end the bull run; the move from Moderate to Max Gain had already happened.
- Verdict: threats outweighed strengths, so the trade was skipped.

## Handling a trade when the market state changes

- A caller had entered on a bull run at around 9:28 and exited flat at 9:44 after the state changed. The speaker called the hold a mistake.
- The rule given, in order: know the exact point value of your stop loss when you enter; when the scenario changes, ask whether the change favours you; then check whether the stop distance has grown or shrunk.
- A shrinking stop means the scenario is in your favour. A growing stop means it is against you, and you exit immediately.
- A support that shifts down does not change the target but widens the stop, which is what makes a rising percentage a weakness for a long trade.

## What the video tells you to do

- Before entering, list strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats for that specific setup, and compare it with the other live setup rather than judging it alone.
- Use these questions as the checklist: what is the target-to-stop-loss ratio in points; is the direction favourable; if the scenario changes, does it help or hurt; is the momentum to that level already complete; is the percentage rising or falling.
- Do not trade where no line exists. The speaker's analogy: a train stops at a station, not at a red signal, so wait for the level instead of anticipating one.
- Exit on a scenario change without waiting to be proved right, especially when the stop distance has widened.
- Redo the analysis from scratch every time the market state flips.
