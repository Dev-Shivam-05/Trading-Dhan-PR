## In one line

Support and resistance no longer work on their own because everyone can now see the same levels, so entries should be taken at six specific points instead — the core idea the speaker calls the Six Kinds of Reversals.

## The problem being described

- Traders buy a Put the moment price touches resistance, and a Call the moment it touches support. The level then stretches past that point and the stop loss is hit.
- After being stopped out, the common reactions both fail: trading the breakout instead, or removing the stop loss entirely because of "stop loss hunting" videos.
- Removing the stop loss turns a small loss into a held losing position. Traders then avoid opening their demat account, wait for expiry to erase the position, and only check the balance after a green day.
- Repeated call-put-call-put entries generate brokerage on every attempt, which is the one certain outcome of the cycle.

## Why the old levels stopped working

- Support and resistance became popular between the two World Wars, when prices were known only two days late and were plotted by hand on paper.
- Computers, the internet, and mobile phones removed that delay. Discount brokers replaced traditional brokers and gave everyone tick-by-tick prices on a phone.
- A "tick" is the smallest price increment a market moves in.
- Because every trader now sees the same levels, volume at those levels is far higher. The market still reverses near support and resistance, but it first **extends** past them — which is exactly where stops sit.

## The six points

Three points open a trade, three close it.

| Open a trade at | Close that trade at |
|---|---|
| Extension of resistance | Diversion |
| Extension of support | Diversion |
| End of diversion | Support or resistance |

- Every level has a paired one: resistance has an extension of resistance, support has an extension of support, and a diversion has an end of diversion.
- At an extension of resistance the trade can be a Put buy, a Call write, or a short in cash or futures. At an extension of support it is the Call side.
- The distance between a level and its extension is not fixed. The speaker gives rough scale: about 20 paise on a ₹100 stock, about ₹10 on a ₹1,000 stock, roughly 25–35 points on Nifty near 20,000.
- The speaker states this distance is derived from Option Greeks and is shown in one click in the LTP Calculator.

## What the video tells you to do

- Do not enter the moment price reaches support or resistance. Wait for the extension.
- Book profit when price reaches a diversion, then wait for a fresh signal rather than staying in.
- Size the position to the point being traded — more aggressive at some, more defensive at others — instead of using one fixed size everywhere.
- Keep the six-point diagram visible at your trading desk. The speaker says it is posted on the channel's Twitter account. The handle is garbled differently across transcripts, so look it up from the channel rather than trusting the spelling here.
