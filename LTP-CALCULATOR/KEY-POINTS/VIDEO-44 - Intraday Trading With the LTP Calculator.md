## The framework he built

- He treats every trade as having a Plan A and a Plan B. Plan A is the profit exit. Plan B is what happens when the trade goes against him, and he argues Plan B is what separates profitable traders from unprofitable ones.
- The model applies to one specific setup: **strong resistance and strong support at the same strike price**, assumed to hold unchanged over a short window rather than a whole day.
- He draws four horizontal lines — resistance and its extension above, support and its extension below — and maps out the paths price can take from the open.
- Price hitting the upper line has two outcomes. He assumes a 50:50 split across ten occurrences: five reverse down (the profitable path), five continue up.
- Of the five that continue up, he assumes a deliberately pessimistic three go further against him and only one or two come back.

## The numbers he assigns

- A correct move from the level to the target is worth roughly **30 points of premium**.
- A trade that goes the wrong way loses about **40 points**, and holding past the next line adds another 10, for **50 points**.
- Over ten occurrences:

| | Safe trader | Risky trader (averages in) |
|---|---|---|
| Best case (5 correct) | +150 points | +150 points |
| Moderate case | 0 to +30 points | +120 points |
| Worst case (3 losses) | −120 points | −60 points |
| **Net** | **+60 points** | **+90 points** |

- With a lot size of 50, that is about ₹3,000 a month for the safe version and ₹4,500 for the risky version, assuming this setup appears about ten times in a 20-day trading month.
- He notes the safe trader's ₹3,000 is close to break-even once brokerage is deducted.
- The risky version differs only in that he adds a second and third lot as price moves against him, then exits at the same final line.

## What the video tells you to do

- Wait for price to reach the defined value before entering. Do not force an entry, and do not exit early on a 10-point adverse move.
- Do not cut the stop loss until price has actually travelled to the third line of the plan.
- Run the same process through at least ten occurrences before judging it. A single day says nothing.
- Start at the safe version. Only widen to averaging in after the process has been shown to work and the execution is consistent.
- Paper trade until you can mark the levels yourself without asking for confirmation, then move to real money.
