# The Retail Trader Mindset That Damaged the Market

## In one line

A thought experiment: if every retail trader in India became profitable at the same time, who would pay for it, and why the system would collapse rather than settle into permanent profit.

## When trading actually is a zero-sum game

- Pure intraday futures and options trading — money in and out the same day — is a zero-sum game. One side's gain is the other side's loss.
- It stops being zero-sum once positional trading, option writing, BTST and cash-segment trading are included, because those participants hold multi-day positions and run strategies where some legs lose and the overall book still profits.
- Even in the zero-sum case both the winner and the loser pay brokerage, so the broker, the exchange and the clearing corporation earn on every trade regardless of outcome. The speaker's phrase for it: every day is a festival for them.
- The exchange is only the middleman. The counterparty to your buy is whoever sold, and the clearing department manages that pairing between two people who never know each other.

## Who currently pays, according to SEBI

- Over FY2022 to FY2024, a SEBI study found roughly 93% of retail F&O traders lost money and about 7% made a profit. Within that 7%, only the top 1% made more than ₹1 lakh.
- Retail losses over those three years totalled about ₹1.8 lakh crore, which went to proprietary trading firms, foreign funds and institutional investors.
- So if retail suddenly started winning, the money would have to flow the other way — out of FII, DII, mutual fund and prop-desk pockets.

## Why universal profit would break down

- Institutions would not sit still while losing. They would return with new strategies, new research and new algorithms, and the cycle would turn back.
- If it did hold, euphoria would set in: people would borrow to trade, shift fixed deposits into equities, and prices would detach from value. With everyone holding and nobody selling, a bubble inflates.
- The moment anyone books profit, others follow, and with no buyers left the bubble bursts into a sharp crash. Late entrants are trapped.
- Liquidity would then dry up completely — no buyers, no sellers. Traders who had been in the market for ten years would leave too.
- Brokers would boom first on enormous volumes and tax collections, then earn nothing at all when the market goes quiet. So even from the broker's side, loss-making traders are structurally necessary.
- The social version: if trading were guaranteed income, engineers, doctors, teachers, factory workers and farmers would all stop working. Production and services would collapse, and GDP with them.
- Government tax revenue would rise briefly on capital gains, then fall, because losing traders currently offset losses against tax while winning ones pay in full only while they are winning.
- SEBI and the exchanges would be forced to intervene, likely with entry barriers or restrictions, to restore stability.

## The speaker's actual conclusion

- A market only functions when views differ. If everyone held the identical view, the market could not run for a single day.
- His analogies: not everyone in an exam can come first, and a recruitment of 1,000 posts cannot work if 100,000 candidates score identically.
- The 10% who win are not lucky. They may have traded only 200 days out of 365, lost on half of those, and still finished the year profitable. Discipline, not frequency, is what separates them — they accept a stop loss as part of the game.
- Long-term investing is the one honest exception. If companies and the index grow, all long-term investors in ETFs, index funds or mutual funds can profit together. Intraday cannot work that way.
- His direct warning: anyone claiming to have built a system that will make every trader in the market profitable is fooling you. He applies the same answer to his own tool when asked what happens if everyone uses the LTP Calculator.
