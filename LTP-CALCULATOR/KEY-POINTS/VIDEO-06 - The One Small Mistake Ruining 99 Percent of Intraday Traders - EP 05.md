## The definition of resistance

Stated as: from the pair of strike prices on the imaginary line, on the call side, start at the **smaller** of the two strikes and move outwards towards out-of-the-money strikes. The first thing you meet — the highest open interest or the highest volume, whichever is closer to the imaginary line — is the resistance on the call side.

- He posted this definition on Twitter (@DaddyInvesting) on 24 August, in Hindi and English.
- Why start at the smaller strike: the call side has already pushed the line up to that point, so searching behind it is pointless. Only the strikes still ahead can stop the move.
- Why it matters: the market cannot keep rising indefinitely. A ball thrown up hits the lid; the strike with enough traded weight behind it is that lid.

## Why this is the mistake in the title

- That resistance volume was created by call buyers themselves — every lot bought, profit booked or loss booked adds to it.
- Their own trading builds the level that later reverses the market against them. This is the branch-and-axe point again.
- The claim is that most intraday traders lose because they do not know where the branch breaks. The speaker says the LTP Calculator computes that value for you, so you exit at the point where the reversal is actually due.

## Questions answered in the video

- *What is out of the money?* Strikes whose premium will be zero after expiry — the ones with no money left in them.
- *Can resistance break?* Yes, and so can support. The useful question is whether it will break, and he claims that can be known well in advance.
- *Can an in-the-money strike be the resistance?* Yes. The rule is only ever "whichever you meet first, starting from the smaller strike".

## What the video tells you to do

- Apply the definition mechanically: find the pair on the imaginary line, start from the smaller strike, move outwards, and take the first of highest volume or highest open interest.
- Say out loud what the level is made of — volume, open interest, or both — every time you identify one.
- Practise on many stocks rather than one, and report unfamiliar cases in the comments.
