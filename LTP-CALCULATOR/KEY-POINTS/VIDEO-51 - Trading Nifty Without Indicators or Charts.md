## The three numbers everything is built from

- The option chain has only three pieces of raw exchange data: **LTP** (last traded price), **volume** and **Open Interest**. Everything else — change in LTP, change in OI, implied volatility, the Greeks — is calculated from those.
- Volume is public-driven: a figure of 83,000 at the 22600 strike divided by the lot size of 75 gives the number of lots actually traded there.
- Open Interest measures how much writing is open at a strike.
- The tool's analysis runs for **six minutes** in the morning and outputs four lines.

## The four 9:20 lines, top to bottom

| Line | Trade | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Extension of resistance plus one | Moderate Put | Small stop loss |
| Extension of resistance | Risky Put | Large stop loss |
| Extension of support | Risky Call | Large stop loss |
| Extension of support minus one | Moderate Call | Small stop loss |

- "Risky" refers to the size of the stop, not to how often it is hit.
- If price moves against a position taken at an inner line, that is not a stop — it is the point to average at the outer line.
- Cost of the risky Call in this session: the two lines were about 50 spot points apart, roughly 25 points of option premium, about ₹1,800 on a 75 lot. If that figure is not acceptable, wait for the outer line instead.

## The trade that appeared on 24 February

- Extension of support sat at **22,547**. Resistance was around 23,000 and support around 22,600 — a 400-point gap, which the speaker reads as a consolidated day.
- Price drifted down through the morning and touched 22,541 around 10:24, giving the risky Call entry.
- **Finding the target.** Take the line's value (547), look on the **opposite** side of the chain for the nearest matching reversal price. The nearest value was 527 on the Put side; the first reversal price visible on its Call side was **577**. That is the target — roughly 70 points from entry, reached within about ten minutes.
- The speaker's point about target size: the calculator only shows the move the chain can currently see. A small visible target means a small move, and taking it is correct.
- **Finding the stop loss.** From the level value 496, find the nearest Put-side value, 481, step down one divergence to 430, then subtract 10 points. The stop was **420**.
- Only one 9:20 trade appeared all day. Price never reached extension of resistance, extension of resistance plus one, or extension of support minus one, so no second trade was taken.

## The live-line reading of the same day

- Early morning: support shifted from bottom to top and resistance shifted up to 23,000 and strengthened. Both shifting upward reads as a bull run scenario — but only once price reaches extension of support around 22,550.
- Around 9:48 resistance bottomed out and the bullish read ended. Volume went weak towards bottom at 600 and Open Interest at 800, which the speaker calls the normal sequence for a resistance shifting down and then re-forming as weak towards top at the new level.
- From roughly noon the resistance stayed unresolved for about three and a half hours while support held strong on its own. That is a **state of confusion**, and it makes a buy at extension of support (around 22,520), with the exit at the resistance weak-towards-top level around 22,762, or at 2:30, whichever comes first.
- The state-of-confusion trade is only valid while the opposite side stays strong. A support bottom cancels it, and at 1:45 the support bottom had not yet formed, so the trade was live.
- A triple bottom formed on support late in the day, too late to be tradable. The day finished consolidated.

## Applying the same lines to stocks

- The four lines are drawn for individual stocks too, labelled C1, C2, P1 and P2.
- **The pairing rule:** if the day's first touch is on C2, you do not trade P2 that day, and vice versa. The same holds for C1 and P1. Only one side of each pair is tradable.
- The stock screen also shows a **bullish risk rating**: 0 is best, 1 is risky, 2 is highly risky, 3 is the rarest and worst. On a stock rated 3, take no more than two Call buys and two Put buys.
- Examples walked through: Bank of Baroda gave one trade that reached target; Coal India gave one that reached target; Federal Bank came close to its target of 875 but missed, then gave a stop loss, with its bullish risk of 3 flagged as the reason for caution; HDFC AMC started at C1 and never touched C2 or P2, so only that one trade; Escorts carried a risk rating of 2.
- The stated workflow: filter for stocks showing zero bullish risk, then trade the ones highlighted on C1 or C2.

## What the video tells you to do

- Wait for price to reach a line value. That waiting, the speaker argues, is what removes overtrading — there is nothing to do between the lines.
- Read the target from the opposite side of the option chain, not from the chart.
- Average at the outer line rather than cutting the position, and only take an inner line if you can fund that average.
- In a consolidated market, take the small target and get out. Premium decays fast when price sits still.
- On stocks, trade only one line of each C/P pair per day, and prefer zero-risk-rated stocks.
