Rather than reading Delta, Theta and Vega values directly, watch the gap between each strike price and its reversal price — the speaker's claim is that this gap is where the Greeks show up as something you can act on.

## The reversal price gap rule

- Every strike has a reversal price on each side. The baseline expectation:
  - A **call-side** reversal price should sit **above** its strike.
  - A **put-side** reversal price should sit **below** its strike.
- A modest gap in both directions is normal. What matters is the direction and size of the deviation.
- **Both sides pushed down** — the call-side reversal falling below its strike, the put-side reversal unusually far below — means bearish.
- **Both sides pushed up** means bullish.
- **Both sides equal or near-equal** means consolidation, no volatility.
- The gap size measures how strong the pressure is, and the change over time is the signal.

## Worked example: Sensex, 4 June

- At 9:15-9:20 AM, the 74,400 strike showed a reversal price of 74,200 — 200 points below, where it should have been above. The put side was similarly stretched down. Verdict: bearish.
- Price at that point was 74,158. Within minutes it fell further.
- By 9:30 AM the gap had narrowed to about 150 points. By 10:00 AM it stayed near 150. The bearish pressure was easing, and price turned up.
- Total analysis time: five minutes at the open. No volume, no Greeks, no indicator.

## Worked example: a consolidating day, 29 April

- Chosen deliberately as a date nobody would remember, so the outcome could not be known in advance.
- At 9:20 AM the gaps were normal — about 50 points up on the call side and 50 down on the put side. Verdict: no volatility, a consolidated day.
- The gaps narrowed to about 20 points up and widened to 82 down, then returned to roughly 50 each way, then settled to equal by 11:20 AM. Price consolidated in line with it.
- By 12:00 PM the call-side reversal began drifting below its strike. Sensex was noticeably more volatile than Nifty that day, which he reads off the same gaps.

## Using the gap while already in a trade

- The stated practical use is exit timing. If you are long from any method — candlesticks, RSI, a moving average — and the reversal price gap has been holding steady at 50 points above, the position is fine.
- When that gap suddenly reverses or behaves abnormally, it is an early warning that the move is about to turn, and a chance to exit before it does.
- He attributes these sudden shifts to news arriving or about to arrive.

## The second factor: the Weekly Range

- Direction from the Greeks is only half the picture. The Weekly Range gives the week's likely travel, through the **W** button.
- Worked example, Nifty for the week of 20 to 26 May. On Wednesday 20 May the Greeks read close to normal and mildly bearish. The Weekly Range gave resistance near 24,330 and support near 23,200, with price closer to support.
- Third question he adds: where is price coming from? Where was it one day ago, two days ago? Direction of approach matters.
- What happened: 20 May set a low near 23,397, close to the Weekly Range support. Price then rose through 21 May to about 23,859, continued Friday and Monday to about 23,972, and on expiry Tuesday touched the Weekly Range top near 24,000 before closing lower.
- His conclusion: when price starts the week near Weekly Range support, expect it to travel to the top. Once it reaches the top, expect bearish momentum from there — which is exactly what expiry day delivered.
- The combination is the point. The Greeks can read bullish while the Weekly Range position says the move is nearly exhausted.

## What the video tells you to do

- Spend five minutes after the open comparing each near strike with its reversal price on both sides.
- Treat call-side reversal above strike and put-side reversal below strike as normal. Both pushed down is bearish, both pushed up is bullish, both equal is consolidation.
- Track how the gap changes through the day. A narrowing bearish gap means the fall is losing force.
- While holding a position from any method, watch the gap for an abnormal reversal as your exit warning.
- Open the Weekly Range with the W button and note where price sits within it, and where it came from over the previous days.
- Read the two together. Near the bottom of the Weekly Range expect upward travel; at the top expect a turn.
