## The idea behind the range lines

- Standard deviation produces three bands around a moving value. The speaker gives rough hit rates: the first band reverses price about 65% of the time, the second about 95%, the third about 99%. He states these are approximate and that the rule is standard mathematics, not his invention.
- Applied to the market, this gives three resistance lines above (RL1, RL2, RL3) and three support lines below (L1, L2, L3).
- He stresses the calculation is not derived from historical price data. He says it is computed from the option chain and Option Greeks — that is, from time value, which is why a full month's range can be drawn on day one.

## When each range appears

- **Nifty** has weekly expiry, so it gets a **weekly range**. New lines are generated on Thursday evening after the close, around 4:00 pm, and are on the chart when Friday opens.
- **Bank Nifty, Fin Nifty, Midcap Nifty** expire monthly, so they get a **monthly range**.
- **Individual F&O stocks** get a monthly range. The lines for the next month appear the day after the current monthly expiry — with expiry on 28 August, the September lines show from the 29th.
- On the LTP Calculator chart the lines are toggled with the **W** button for weekly and **M** for monthly.

## What the video tells you to do

- For option writing, sell strikes on **both** sides at the same level. L1 on both sides is the most aggressive, L2 is safer, L3 is the safest.
- Treat L1 writing on individual stocks as only moderate risk, because stocks move less than Nifty and Bank Nifty.
- For cash buying, enter at support L1 if you accept more risk, or wait for L2 for a safer entry.
- Start writing from day one of the month, so the whole month's time value works in your favour.
