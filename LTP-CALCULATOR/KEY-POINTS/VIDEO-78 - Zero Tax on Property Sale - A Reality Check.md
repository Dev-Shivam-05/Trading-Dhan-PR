## Why the speaker says "zero tax" is wrong

- The ₹3.85 crore did not vanish — it went to the husband, and it is a sale for him too.
- If his cost on that flat was, say, around ₹1 crore, he is sitting on roughly ₹2.85 crore of gain. Unless he reinvests it, he owes long-term capital gains tax on it.
- So the exemption is not a family-level saving. It simply relocated the liability from wife to husband.
- Worse, the structure costs extra. The wife paid ₹11.55 lakh of stamp duty buying from her husband, and the husband will pay stamp duty again when he reinvests his proceeds — stamp duty twice.
- Had the wife simply bought any other flat from an unrelated seller, she would have got the identical Section 54 exemption with stamp duty paid only once.

## What the video tells you to do

- Check who ends up holding the sale proceeds before believing a "zero tax" claim. A tax that moves to another person is not a tax that was avoided.
- Count stamp duty on both legs when a property deal is routed through a family member.
- Understand a case fully before reacting to a reel about it.
