## How to read the Option Chain layout

- The strike price sits in the middle of the table. Call-side data is on the left, put-side data on the right.
- The first column on each side is the LTP — the premium. The left one is the Call LTP, the right one the Put LTP.
- Example used: the 19,250 strike with a Call premium of ₹83.

## Volume versus open interest

- **Volume** counts trades. Buy one lot and one lot of volume appears at that strike; ten trades in a day add ten lots of volume. The comparison used is a toll booth — you pay every single time you pass, however many times that is.
- **Open interest** counts positions that are still standing.
- A buyer is someone who takes a position by buying first. A **writer** is someone who sells first to create a new position.
- The person selling to you can be either an existing holder squaring off, or a writer opening a fresh position.
  - If the seller is a writer opening a new position, open interest is created.
  - If the seller is an existing holder closing out, volume is generated but open interest is not.
- Open interest rises when writers sell fresh premium and falls when writers buy their positions back.

## Questions answered in the video

- *Do writers run the market?* No. Without buyers no position gets executed and no open interest is created. The comparison used is opening a water shop in the Sahara: the stock is worthless without a customer.
- *Do you watch volume or open interest?* Both. He compares it to starting a car — clutch, gear and accelerator are all needed, each at the right moment. Where each one is applied is taught in later episodes.

## What the video tells you to do

- Before studying support and resistance, learn to tell writers and buyers apart, because volume and open interest cannot be read correctly without it.
- Check whether a rise in volume is matched by a rise in open interest, since the two carry different information.
