## What the 9:20 lines are

- On the LTP Calculator chart there is a **920** button. Clicking it after 9:21 am draws four horizontal lines.
- The lines are generated once, at 9:21 am, and then stay fixed for the whole session. They do not move with price, and they remain in place when you load that date in historical data.
- The four lines on the day of recording (28 August, Nifty near 24,652): **US-1 at 24,529**, **US / EOS (extension of support) at 24,575**, **UR / EOR (extension of resistance) at 24,620**, and **UR+1 at 24,668**.
- The claim is that once the market reverses from these lines and completes its targets, that is the day's range.
- A flow chart of the rules is available inside the tool under the question-mark button.

## The writing strategy

- Precondition: click 920 at or after 9:21. If all four lines are present, trade. If the lines on one side are missing, no trade is created on that side.
- Sell the call strike **above UR+1**: with UR+1 at 24,668, that is the 24,700 call, quoted at about ₹29.
- Sell the put strike **below US-1**: with US-1 at 24,529, that is the 24,500 put, quoted at about ₹8.
- Stop loss is the **combined premium of both legs** — roughly ₹37-38 in this example.
- If one side hits the stop, square off that leg only and let the other decay to zero. The outcome is roughly no profit, no loss.
- If neither side is hit, exit both legs at 2:30 pm. On Thursday (expiry day) hold to 3:30 pm.

## The buying strategy

- **Bullish, risky trader**: buy a call when price reaches US (the extension of support). Then press **Set D** and pick D1, D2 or D3 to get the target and stop loss.
- If price rises, exit at the chosen D target. If price instead falls to **US-1**, average with **double the lot size** — one lot at US, two lots at US-1 — then press Set D again. D1 is the target; the corresponding put-side D1 value is the stop loss.
- **Bullish, moderate trader**: skip the first entry, wait for US-1, press D, set target and stop loss, and exit. No averaging.
- **Bearish**: buy a put at UR. If price keeps rising to **UR+1**, that is either the averaging point for the risky trader or a fresh trade for the moderate one.
- In the live example on the put side, the stop loss came out near 24,711 (D2 call) and the targets were 24,625, then 24,580, then 24,528. The speaker's rule for which target to take: the less experienced you are, the closer you should stop — inexperienced traders take D1 only.

## Why he says the EMA falls short

- The EMA used is a **9-period EMA on a 5-minute chart**, the standard intraday setting.
- An EMA only exists where price has already been. There is no line ahead of the current candle, so it cannot tell you in the morning where the day's top or bottom might be.
- At the bottom that reversed on the day, the EMA gave no buy indication; the 9:20 line did, at 9:21.
- At the top, the EMA was still saying "buy" while the EOR line had already marked a selling level. A trader following the EMA could be stopped out long and then, on the cross below, stopped out short as well — trapped on both sides.
