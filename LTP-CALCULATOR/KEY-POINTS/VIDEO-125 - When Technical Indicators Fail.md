The follow-up to the "where does it fail" argument: a walkthrough of Sensex on 5 June showing that when a support level repeatedly holds and then suddenly breaks, the option chain flagged the change hours in advance.

## The unanswered question from charts

- Draw a resistance line on a chart and price reverses off it several times, then one time it does not. Draw a support line and the same thing happens.
- Candlestick theory offers no reason for the failure. The only response it has is to place a stop loss, which acknowledges the failure without explaining it.
- The speaker's claim is that the explanation is visible on the option chain before the failure happens.

## Reading the option chain in four steps

1. **Spot price** — where the market is currently trading, shown by the red **imaginary line** between two strikes. In the example it sat between 74,000 and 74,100.
2. **Strike prices** — the column down the middle.
3. **Resistance** — the day's resistance, read at the top of the screen.
4. **Support** — the day's support.

- On the day shown, support and resistance were both at 74,500.
- Each of the two levels can be in one of three conditions: strong in itself (neutral), weak towards top (bullish pressure), or weak towards bottom (bearish pressure).
- Colour tells you which: green means bullish pressure, red means bearish pressure.
- The speaker states that support and resistance in his tool are defined by volume and open interest, by his own definition, and once set they are used for the whole day.

## The research gap he claims to have closed

- The naive reading is that the largest volume marks support. But that support breaks often enough to be unreliable.
- His answer: watch the **second** largest volume. When a secondary cluster forms below the main support — in the example, a 78% reading at 74,000 beneath the 74,500 support — that is weak-towards-bottom, and it opens the door to a 500-point fall.
- The signal appears the moment the secondary volume forms, not at the moment price breaks. So the warning is available early.

## The 5 June walkthrough

- 9:15-9:20 AM: support 74,500 and strong in itself, resistance weak towards top. Banner reads bullish.
- 9:53 AM: resistance still weak towards top, support turns strong. The bull run downgrades to a neutral scenario.
- 10:19-10:20 AM: banner reads **Bullish SOC** (Bullish State of Confusion). The direction is bullish, but no entry is available yet.
- Candlestick reading at this moment would say buy the support that has reversed price several times already. AI LTP says buy nothing, and wait for 74,300 — roughly 250 points below the then-current price of about 74,583.
- The buy level is not arbitrary. It is the reversal price calculated at the strike holding the highest open interest sitting under the highest volume. Nearer reversal prices exist, but only the one at the heaviest strike is offered.
- No sell level is given at all, because resistance is weak towards top, meaning bullish. The speaker's point: selling that resistance would fail repeatedly, and a chart gives you no reason why.
- Around 11:00 AM price falls to the buy zone, holds above the S Max stop level, and turns. The move arrives.
- 11:10 AM onwards: the bullish scenario persists through 11:20, 11:48 and 12:52, and price returns to the same buy zone. The tool still shows it, but the rule says do not take it.

## The second-touch rule

- The rule: trade a level once. Do not take a second trade at the same level.
- How he says he arrived at it: he traded the setup, reviewed the losses, found that S Risky and S Moderate entries were failing on repeat touches, and concluded that a level weakens once it has been hit. The rule was then written in to close that gap.
- This is offered as the working example of the research method described in the previous episode — trade the idea, catalogue the failures, isolate the condition, and add a rule that forbids it.

## When the scenario finally flipped

- Later in the day a new volume cluster built below support. The tool left it alone until it crossed a significant percentage, then highlighted it.
- Support went from weak-towards-top-too-strong (already bearish pressure, flagged since morning) to **weak towards bottom** — meaning the level was no longer just under pressure but actively moving down.
- The banner changed from Bullish SOC to both-sides-risky. Buying and selling both became risky.
- A new S Risky buy level appeared at 73,920 with a stop just below at 73,824. The instruction is to wait for that price and nothing else.

## What the video tells you to do

- Read the screen in order: spot price, strike prices, resistance, support, then the condition and colour of each.
- Do not buy at a support that a chart shows reversing repeatedly. Wait for the reversal price at the strike with the highest open interest and highest volume.
- Treat green as bullish pressure and red as bearish pressure on the level.
- Never sell into a resistance marked weak towards top, and never buy into a support marked weak towards bottom.
- Take one trade per level per scenario. When the level is hit again in the same scenario, stand aside.
- Wait for the scenario banner to change before looking for the next trade, and then use the new levels it produces.
- Watch for a secondary volume cluster forming below support or above resistance. That is the early warning that the level will break.
