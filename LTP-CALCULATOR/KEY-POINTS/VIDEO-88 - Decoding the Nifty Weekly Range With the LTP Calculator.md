## Where the range comes from

- Every buyer and writer already trades with a range in mind: a target on one side and a stop on the other. Open interest and volume build up at particular strikes because participants are collectively fixing the week's or month's range.
- That range depends on the gap between spot and futures prices, and on the Greeks that gap generates (delta, theta, vega, gamma, rho and the minor Greeks), which the speaker treats as a read on trader sentiment and activity.
- The levels are calculated on standard-deviation rules, not on historical price data or trend lines.
- Reading the option chain directly is described as too complicated to do by hand: open interest and volume keep shifting between strikes all day. The tool's system watches the market from 9:15 to 3:30 and again at the 4:00 settle, and generates the range from that.

## The key property: the levels are static

- The weekly range is calculated once, at 4:00 pm on the previous Tuesday expiry, and then the lines are fixed for the whole week. They do not move as price moves.
- Press **W** on the chart for Nifty's weekly range, **M** on a stock for its monthly range. The monthly range starts the day after the previous month's expiry.
- Three bands sit on each side: L1, L2 and L3. The speaker gives their hit rates as roughly 65% for L1, roughly 95% for L2, and above 99% for L3, which he says almost never gets reached.

## What the video tells you to do

- Write paired strikes: the call side L1 against the put side L1, taking strikes just outside the range levels.
- On day one and day two, premiums often spike when the market jumps. Do not chase them; hold the position and trust the fixed range.
- If you find yourself trading out at L2, treat that as something caused by a large gap-up rather than normal behaviour.
- Once a stock is sitting on a monthly range line, watch it for the rest of the month to see whether it closes back inside L1 or pushes towards L2.
