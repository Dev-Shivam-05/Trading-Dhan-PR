# Indicators vs Option Chain - Not Every Influencer Is a Researcher

## In one line

A test for judging anyone who teaches trading: a genuine researcher tells you where their method fails, while an influencer only shows you the trades where it worked.

## The argument about research

- Existing research is everything already published and freely learnable: Japanese candlestick patterns, bullish engulfing, Heikin Ashi, EMA, RSI, Bollinger Bands. Repeating it is not research, and you need no teacher for it.
- Real research starts by identifying the **gap** in the existing work — the conditions under which it stops functioning.
- Two analogies used. A laptop works well until ambient temperature exceeds 40°C, when it overheats and shuts down; that limit is the gap. An electric vehicle matches a petrol car for 300 km but then needs four to six hours to charge; closing that to five minutes is the research problem.
- His conclusion: every product and every method has a boundary. Finding it is the work.
- On who to follow: he argues anyone teaching finance should have watched the market for at least a decade first, and that viewers should be selective rather than following whoever has the most followers.

## The EMA demonstration

- He deliberately builds the case *for* EMA first. A 30-period exponential moving average is a single line: buy when a candle closes above it, sell when a candle closes below.
- On the chart he steps through close after close where the rule produced a profitable move, and asks why anyone would bother with a complex option chain tool.
- He explains the mechanic: a 30 EMA averages the last 30 candles, but the most recent five to seven carry extra weight, which is what makes it exponential rather than simple.
- Then he shows the other cases: closes above the line that were immediately followed by a decline, and closes below that reversed up. Each one is a booked loss.
- The point is not that EMA is bad. It is that no video he has seen bothers to identify when the close-above signal fails.

## The same test applied to his own tools

- He says he applies this to his own work, and talks more about when 920 fails and when Chart 1.0 fails than about when they work.
- A stated example of a failure condition in his own method: in a bullish market standing at its top, he will not buy at the top. The trade is only taken from the bottom. Taking it at the top repeatedly is where the loss comes from.
- His framing of the payoff: once you filter out the failure conditions, the valid entries appear on their own, even if that leaves you only two trades in a month.

## Why he moved from candlesticks to the option chain

- He started on candlestick patterns and grew tired of them because nothing in the pattern explained its own failures.
- A live example from the day's chart: a tweezer bottom formed at a support level and reversed, producing a good move. Later the same level produced a second tweezer bottom which broke down instead. Candlestick theory offers no answer for why the same pattern at the same level behaved differently.
- His claim is that the answer exists in the option chain, where volume, open interest at each strike, and the Greeks including implied volatility are all visible. Showing that is deferred to episode two.

## How the LTP Calculator started

- The first prototype was entirely manual. He pulled the option chain from the NSE website, took the market price, the call-side LTP and the put-side LTP at a strike, and the Greeks — delta, theta, vega, gamma — from wherever a broker terminal exposed them.
- He typed all of it into a calculator and pressed calculate, which returned the reversal price for that strike.
- The purpose was narrow: get the exact price at which to enter. His example is a Sensex Put trade at the 74,500 strike where the reversal price came out at 46, so he waited for 46 and bought the Put there.
- All of that is now automatic, with a reversal price shown at every strike.

## What the video tells you to do

- Before adopting any indicator, find the conditions under which it fails, and stop trading it in those conditions.
- Judge a teacher by whether they discuss their failures. If they only show winners, treat it as repackaged existing research.
- When a pattern works at a level once and fails at the same level later, treat that as the question to investigate rather than an accident.
- Accept a small number of trades per month as the cost of filtering properly.
