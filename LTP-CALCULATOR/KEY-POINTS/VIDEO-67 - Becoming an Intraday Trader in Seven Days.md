## The first lesson: direction and which option to buy

- Two moves were marked on the chart — one down, one up. Money is made in both, provided you are on the correct side.
- Profit always comes from buying lower and selling higher; on a fall, you sell first and buy back lower.
- So: market expected to rise, buy a **Call**; market expected to fall, buy a **Put**.
- Watching the option chain live confirms it. As spot moved from around 25,543 up towards 25,550, the 25,500 call premium rose from about ₹63 to ₹65-66 while the put premium fell from about ₹28 downwards.

## Why people buy options instead of futures

- The Nifty futures value shown was about 25,636 with a lot size of 75 units, so a full lot is worth roughly ₹19.22 lakh.
- The exchange does not demand the full amount. Margin is roughly a tenth, about ₹1.25 lakh, and that is the minimum balance needed to trade one futures lot.
- Someone without that capital buys a **portion** of the move instead: an option. A ₹62 premium on 75 units costs about ₹4,650 rather than lakhs, and your profit and loss is calculated on that ₹4,650.
- The exam analogy used: eight students splitting an eight-paper exam each write only one paper, so each one's marks are only their own share of the total. Buying an option is buying one paper, not the whole exam.
- Cheaper, far-out premiums move less for the same index move; costlier, nearer premiums move more. On a 100-point index move, one strike might gain 50 while another gains 80 and a further one only 10.

## How much the premium actually moves — delta

- In the LTP Calculator's Greeks view, the student was shown delta alone, with gamma, theta and vega switched off.
- The total move for a ₹1 change in the index is ₹1, split across the two sides. In the example the call side carried 60 paise and the put side 40 paise.
- So a ₹1 rise adds 60 paise to the call premium and takes 40 paise off the put; a ₹1 fall reverses it.
- The strike price is the dividing line. The left side gains as the market rises, the right side gains as the market falls.

## What the video tells you to do

- Pick one strike near the money and watch only the call and put figures that face each other at that same strike. Comparing across different strikes will confuse you.
- Write down the current call premium, the put premium and the spot price as a baseline, then wait until the premium has moved 10 to 15 rupees before judging what happened. Do not try to read it on every tick.
- Do this as the day's practice: watch and observe rather than trade.
- Turn off all Greeks except delta while learning what the premium does.
