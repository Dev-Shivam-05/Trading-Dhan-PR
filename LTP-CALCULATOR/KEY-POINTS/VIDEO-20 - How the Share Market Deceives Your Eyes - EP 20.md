## The challenger idea

- Through the day a strong support or resistance gets challenged by another strike trying to take its place. The speaker compares it to sledging in cricket: the challenger's job is to provoke.
- Because of that, the market often travels to the challenger's level, which is why the chain can look like one thing and resolve as another.

## First touch versus second touch

- At the 9:15–9:17 open, resistance was strong and support was weak towards the bottom; the support strengthened as the morning went on.
- Extension of resistance sat around 19,844 and the chart made its high of 19,842 at 9:55 — a clean reversal at the level.
- Strong support plus strong resistance is **not** a safe trade. A support that was weak towards the bottom earlier can still let the market make one extra divergence. The video insists on keeping "safe" and "risky" apart.
- Count the touches. The market opened at the extension of support, so the open itself was the first touch. It came back around 10:40 for the second. The first touch is the safe one; from the second the market has room to fall further.
- A trade taken from the extension of resistance is carried only to the midpoint, booking profit on each divergence, never held on the assumption that resistance will be reached.

## The compare feature

- Click the volume on the put side of a strike and a **compare** option appears; select the next strike down and add it.
- The percentage that comes back is the reading. In the example, 19,700 compared against 19,600 sat near 75, which the speaker treats as confirmation of a move towards the bottom.

## Two rules that agreed

- With strong support being hit a second time, the target is the extension of support minus one strike price — the 19,750 divergence, about 19,726–19,727.
- With the support turning weak towards the bottom at 19,700, the target is that strike plus one — again the 19,750 divergence.
- Both rules producing the same number fixed the day's low. The market hit roughly 19,726–19,733 repeatedly from 11:15 am to about 12:10 pm without breaking it.
- Because the floor was confirmed twice, buying calls there was a safe bullish trade, with the extension of resistance near 19,830 as the destination.
- The point the video is built around: without the weak-towards-bottom reading, a second touch of support would have meant an extra divergence down. The weakness appearing at the immediately adjacent strike reverses that and holds the market up. "Look somewhere, target somewhere."
- If the weakness instead appears a divergence further away — at 19,750 rather than 19,700 — the arithmetic moves with it and the plus-one target becomes the extension of support itself.

## What the video tells you to do

- Count how many times the market has already touched the level before trading it.
- Use the compare feature on adjacent strikes to confirm whether a support is genuinely weakening.
- Take the trade when the second-touch rule and the weak-towards-bottom rule point at the same level; treat that as the day's floor.
- Book profits divergence by divergence rather than assuming the opposite level will be reached.
- Exit all option-chain-based trades by 2:30 pm. After that the readings are described as chaotic.
