## In one line

Three readings taken on the first morning of a new expiry - the weekly range, the implied volatility level and where support and resistance have shifted - used to call the coming week's size, volatility and bias.

## The three questions being answered

- How many points will Nifty travel this week, up or down?
- Will the week be more or less volatile than the one before it?
- Will the pressure be bullish or bearish?

All three are read on the first trading day of a new expiry, from the 9:15 am Option Chain, using the LTP Calculator's weekly range feature and its historical data to compare weeks.

## How each reading works

- **Range size.** The weekly range gives a top and a bottom. Subtract one from the other and that number is the distance Nifty is expected to cover during the week.
- **Volatility.** Read implied volatility around the at-the-money strike and compare it with the previous week's opening reading. A higher number means a wider, faster week.
- **Direction.** Compare this week's support and resistance with last week's. Both levels higher means bullish pressure; both lower means bearish pressure.
- Implied volatility never gives direction on its own - it only tells you how big the moves will be. Direction comes from the level comparison and the Chart of Accuracy reading.

## The four weeks compared

- **11 January expiry** (opened 5 January): range 21,885 to 21,432, about 453 points. IV near 10. Nifty actually ran from about 21,450 to 21,775, roughly 325 points - comfortably inside the range.
- **18 January expiry** (opened 12 January): range 21,905 to 21,390, about 515 points, IV 11 to 11.5. Both resistance and support had moved higher than the previous week, so the bias was bullish. Nifty made its all-time high that week and still closed inside the range.
- **25 January expiry** (opened 19 January): IV opened at 14.6-14.7. Range 21,780 to 21,145, about 635 points. Resistance had dropped from 905 to 780 and support from 390 to 145 - both lower, so bearish pressure. Price opened at the top of the range, worked down to the bottom over three days and closed back inside.
- **1 February expiry** (opened 29 January, the recording day): range 21,680 to 21,025, about 655 points. Resistance down again from 780 to 680 with support lower too, so mild bearish pressure. IV opened near 17, was 19-20 by mid-morning and 21-23 by about 1:25 pm. Nifty printed a high near 21,760 and closed around 21,737, sitting above the top of its own weekly range.

## The point about big candles

- Big candles are not produced by high IV by itself, but by a sharp jump in IV within the week.
- The 25 January week jumped from 10-11 to 14-15 and produced several unusually large Nifty candles. The recording day jumped from 17 to 20-plus and produced another.
- A week where IV stays flat tends to give gap-ups and gap-downs with consolidation in between, rather than long candles.

## What the video tells you to do

- Pull the 9:15 am data on the first day of a new expiry and note three things: range top, range bottom, and at-the-money IV.
- Use the historical data feature to pull the same three numbers for the previous week and compare them side by side.
- Treat the range figure as the expected week's travel and expect price to close back inside it even after breaking out intraday.
- Watch IV change through the day, not just at the open - a sharp rise is the warning that large candles are coming.
