## Why a fixed-point stop loss fails

- A 20-point stop is a number the trader chose. The market does not know it and nobody else is trading around it.
- Because the loss looks small - 20 points times a lot of 75 is about Rs 1,500 - the trade feels easy, so the trader only plans the favourable case.
- When price goes against the position, the stop gets moved to the extension of support, then to the next diversion below, and the planned Rs 1,500 loss becomes 150 or 220 points.
- Eight winning trades of 50 points and two losses of that size leave the account flat or negative. The speaker walks the guest through her own numbers showing exactly this pattern.
- A stop loss has to come from market structure - where support and resistance sit and which way the pressure runs - not from a number you like.

## How support and resistance are defined here

- Take the current price as an "imaginary line" and the pair of strike prices immediately above and below it.
- For support, start from the higher strike of that pair on the put side and move outward to lower strikes. The first strike carrying 100% volume or 100% open interest, whichever is nearer the imaginary line, is support.
- Support therefore can never sit more than one strike in the money.
- Resistance is the mirror image: start from the lower strike of the pair on the call side and move up to higher strikes.
- Support formed at 9:15 stays valid for the day even after price trades below it, provided the largest open interest at that strike has not unwound. Bank Nifty's 58,000 support held its status all session while price fell through it.
- Practice cases from the video: a pharma stock supported at 1,140 by volume alone with open interest far away; BSE supported at 2,400 where the highlighted 83% figure was not the real level; Divi's Labs at 6,800 rather than the 92% strike at 7,000; Maruti resistance 16,000, support 15,500.

## Reversal prices and how far a level can stretch

- The app prints a reversal price against every strike. The speaker says he derives it from the Greeks - Delta, Theta, Vega, Gamma, Rho - plus implied volatility, both sides' last traded prices, spot and futures.
- A level's reach is set by its reversal price, not by the strike. Bank Nifty support at 58,000 had a reversal price near 57,700, meaning price could drop roughly 300 points below the strike and the level would still be doing its job.
- The day's low came in near 57,528, one further diversion below, and support was still counted as 58,000.
- ICICI Bank showed the same thing: largest open interest at 1,350 formed when price was near 1,341 in the morning, reversal price near 1,336, and the day's low arrived around there.
- A trader reading only the highest open interest would give up long before price reached the level that actually mattered.

## What the video tells you to do

- Stop setting stop losses in fixed points. Derive every stop from the level structure of that particular day.
- Always cross-check the support and resistance values printed at the top of the app against the strike you picked by eye. The guest misread several stocks by skipping this step.
- Track open interest from 9:15 onwards rather than judging the chain at the moment you happen to open it.
- Keep written notes of each lesson as the season progresses.
