## In one line

A walk-through of Chart of Accuracy 2.0, the speaker's nine-scenario reading of how call-side and put-side Open Interest move together, used to decide direction when the market is stuck in a range.

## The problem it is meant to solve

- A consolidating market moves sideways in a flat line and gives no clue whether the next move is towards a higher or a lower strike price.
- This happens in two places: after price has reached its extensions, and in mid-market when price sits between its divergences.
- It also covers the second hit. The first time price hits an extension of resistance or extension of support, the reversal trade is the normal one. On a repeat hit the reversal becomes risky, and this tool is used to judge it.
- Explicit warning: do not use Chart of Accuracy 2.0 on the first hit of an extension. The speaker says it will point the opposite way and talk you out of a valid trade.

## Where it sits in the LTP Calculator

- The chart is built automatically inside the LTP Calculator. Clicking "OI Change" on either the call side or the put side opens it.
- The point of it is to replace reading Option Chain numbers with reading two lines on a graph: a red line for put-side Open Interest and a green line for call-side Open Interest.
- The live example is 1 December, with the 7 December Nifty expiry, recorded around 3 pm. Price was stalling around 20,290, between the 20,250 and 20,300 strikes, and the 20,300 extension was reading near 290.

## The nine scenarios

1. Both lines flat and parallel - consolidation, price stays stuck.
2. Call OI rising sharply, put OI flat - bearish from the divergence of that strike.
3. Call OI falling, put OI flat - mildly bullish from the divergence of that strike.
4. Call OI flat, put OI rising sharply - mildly bullish from the strike where put OI is rising.
5. Both rising sharply - price stays boxed between the two strikes. It breaks below the call-side divergence and rises above the put-side divergence. Expect roughly 50 points of range in Nifty, 100 in Bank Nifty, and the strike gap in stocks.
6. Call OI falling sharply against a flat put side - a strong bullish run off that strike's divergence.
7. Put OI falling steeply, call OI flat - price falls from that strike.
8. Call OI rising while put OI falls - price falls off that strike's divergence.
9. Both lines falling - the whole market consolidates until a direction appears.

## What the video tells you to do

- Read the chart only when price has been range-bound for hours, or on a second extension hit - never on the first hit.
- Identify the strike price at which the described OI behaviour is happening. That strike's divergence is the level the move starts from.
- On scenario 5, treat the two strikes as the boundaries of a range rather than looking for a trend.
