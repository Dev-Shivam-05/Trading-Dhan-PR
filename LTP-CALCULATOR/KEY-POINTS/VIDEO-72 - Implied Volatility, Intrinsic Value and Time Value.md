## Why the move produced no profit

- The 25,200 put premium was about ₹55, with a delta near 0.55. A 40-point rise in the index should therefore add only about ₹20 to it.
- By the time that ₹20 arrives, time value has already drained out of the premium. So when the index went to 25,200 and came back to 25,170, the ₹55 premium came back at only about ₹45 — less than it started.
- Tracked live: ₹55, then ₹50, then ₹48, then ₹44 as the index returned to the same 25,165–25,168 level it had left.
- The realistic gain was seven to ten points — enough to be eaten by brokerage. The conclusion was to trade for that gap is not worth it.

## The trap in the deep strike

- The 25,300 put had **zero time value**, so it was expected to move one for one with the index, like a futures position. That looked like the one exception worth trading.
- It was not. When the index rose 40 points, about ₹14 of time value was **added** into that premium, and when the index fell back, that added time value was taken out again.
- Net result: the 25,300 put went from around ₹135 to ₹125 while the index returned to the same place. A buyer of either strike lost money.
- The speaker's line on it: the market consumed the time value of the 25,300 strike, handed back ₹2, and put the index back where it started.

- On the call side the reversal prices sat almost on top of the strikes — 25,200 showed 25,205, 25,250 showed 25,250, 25,300 showed about 25,300, 25,350 showed 25,350. Almost no time value, and nothing attractive on the bullish side.

- At expiry all reversal prices converge to intrinsic value: with the index at 25,198, the 25,200 strike leaves about ₹2 on one side and nothing on the other. So if the market consolidates or drifts up to 25,250, the bearish premium simply decays to nothing as the put reversal prices climb back towards the strike.

## What the video tells you to do

- Check implied volatility and time value before buying any premium. If time value is already gone, a buyer's edge is gone with it.
- Work out the expected move in the premium from delta before entering: a 40-point index move on a 0.55 delta is only about ₹20, and time decay will take part of that.
- Expect a zero-time-value strike to gain time value on a move up and lose it on the way back, so it does not behave like futures over a round trip.
- In this kind of market either take the move in futures, where a 30-point fall actually pays 20–25 points, or do not trade at all.
- Wait for the level you have decided on — the discussion settled on doing nothing unless the index reached 25,250.
- Strip the chart down to the option chain and delta when analysing the premium itself.
