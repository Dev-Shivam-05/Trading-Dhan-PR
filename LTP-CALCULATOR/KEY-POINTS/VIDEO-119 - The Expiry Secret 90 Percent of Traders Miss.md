# The Expiry Secret 90 Percent of Traders Miss

## In one line

A live classroom session in which students propose methods for predicting where the market will close on expiry day, and the speaker tests each one until only the Weekly Range method survives.

## The question being tested

- The session runs at about 11:30 AM on a weekly expiry day, with Nifty near 22,900. The challenge to the room is to name a single closing number for 3:30 PM, not a range and not a probability.
- The speaker's framing: expiry day has one purpose, which is to terminate the expiring weekly contracts and drive out-of-the-money premiums to zero.
- Each student method is examined and, in most cases, rejected for a stated reason.

## Methods proposed and why most were rejected

- **Change in LTP (Change PTS), both sides.** If calls are green the market goes up; if puts are green it goes down; if both are falling the market is stuck. Both sides can never be green at once. On this day both were red, so the signal gave nothing. The speaker attributes that to roughly ₹150 of time value being consumed by the gap-down open.
- **Open Interest change.** The idea is that the market moves towards the side where OI change is negative, or settles between the two heaviest OI strikes (example given: about 32,000 on the 22,800 put and about 36,000 on the 23,000 call) so sellers keep the most. Rejected because OI unwinds within minutes; those positions "run away" as soon as price moves.
- **Delta.** A student suggests that a strike with delta 0.6 or above is unlikely to expire at zero — the example given was 0.69 delta at 22,850. The speaker calls this correct but bookish: it gives a probability, not a closing number.
- **Nifty futures consolidation.** Rejected because spot and futures values only converge on monthly expiry, not weekly.
- **Time value.** A student argues that if the market touched support and support-1 before 9:30 or 10:30 it usually travels up to resistance, and predicts a close above a divergence of resistance. The speaker lets this stand as a reasonable line of logic.
- **Historical data.** Looking at past 3:30 PM data usually shows both sides at the same strike, and it only tells you at 3:00 PM, which is too late to trade.

## The method that survived: Weekly Range

- The Weekly Range screen shows six levels, three on each side of price.
- The market tends to close near whichever of those levels is nearest to the current price.
- On the day in question, price at 22,900 was about 200 points from 23,108 and about 1,400 points from 21,554. A 200-point move in four hours is plausible; a 1,400-point move is not. So 23,108 was the answer the speaker accepted.
- Caveat given on the spot: the market can do anything. He recalls 17 April, when Nifty was about 900 points from a level at midday and still reached it by 3:30, with roughly 300 points coming in a 15-20 minute burst after 1:30 PM.

## How the speaker actually manages a trade

- If a trade does not work within 15-20 minutes, he closes it regardless of whether it is at a small profit or a small loss. He states plainly that he is not good at holding, and that his job is to stop the loss, not to capture the target.
- He describes taking a three-point profit on an options trade after moving the stop to cost, because the position had stalled. He defends this: the same trade can be re-entered later.
- He prefers repeating one setup at one level (the example used: trading from the same 924 level and averaging if it recurs) over hunting a new method every day. If a setup works 15-16 times out of 20, there is no need for a new one.
- He says he does not trade diversions at all.

## What the video tells you to do

- On expiry day, open the Weekly Range and take the level closest to current price as the likely closing area.
- Judge a candidate level by how many points away it is and how many hours remain. Discard a level that needs an implausible move.
- Do not rely on OI as a closing predictor — it changes too fast.
- Enter after the pull in one direction ends, not while it is in progress: for a Call, wait for the downward pull from support to finish.
- Trade the first two hours of your session only. The speaker says the brain operates at full capacity in the market for roughly two hours, after which forcing more analysis leads to being trapped.
- Fix a loss limit before entry. His stated example: risk 30 points for a 300-point target.
