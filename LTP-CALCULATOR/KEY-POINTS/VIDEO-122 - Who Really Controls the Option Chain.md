## Where price comes from

- The answer to the title question is that traders themselves set the price. When a buyer and a seller agree on a trade, that becomes the Last Traded Price, and the LTP is the market price.
- Gold at ₹1,50,000 means the last deal was struck there. When it reads ₹1,45,000, a later deal set that. Nifty works the same way, tick by tick between 9:15 AM and 3:30 PM.
- Three markets trade the same underlying: spot is today's price, futures is the price for later, options is that value broken into parts. The speaker tells beginners to set all three aside and watch only one thing — the number on screen and whether it goes up or down.
- Trading is buying lower and selling higher, and you may sell first and buy back later. A sell at 22,773 covered at 22,763 is a profit; covered at 22,783 it is a loss.

## Why knowing the direction changes everything

- His gold illustration: ask a room whether gold is expensive today and most say yes. Then tell them a year from now it will be ₹5 lakh, and the same price suddenly looks cheap. Tell them it will fall, and it looks expensive.
- The point: "expensive" and "cheap" are not properties of the price. They are conclusions you draw once you have a view on direction. Real estate is his stated exception, where he says direction cannot usefully be guessed.

## Reading a candle, and what you actually need to know

- A candle shows four things for its period: open, high, low and close. The example used is a 5-minute candle opening at 22,761 with a high near 22,781.
- The useful question is not "will price go up or down" but **which of the two extremes gets hit first**. If price reaches 22,781 and turns, sell there. If it drops to 22,715 and turns, buy there.
- The upper turning level is resistance, the lower one is support. Price generally travels between them and tests each repeatedly.

## Why the option chain, and not indicators

- Nobody can see who is buying and who is selling. His analogy: ask a hall to write their market view on a slip and hide it — there is no way to count them.
- The exchange and regulator deliberately prevent that data existing. Some participants use colocation servers precisely so their trades stay unidentifiable.
- What is available is snapshot data published after trades execute. That is the option chain, and it is what the speaker's research is built on.
- Most standard indicators — moving averages, Fibonacci, the level indicators, 52-week high and low, accumulation/distribution, ADX — derive from historical price data. The speaker's stated distinction is that his work is built on the live LTPs in the option chain instead.
- Terminology he claims to have introduced: the **imaginary line**, the red marker showing where the market price sits between strikes. He says no broker terminal displayed this until the LTP Calculator did about three years earlier, and that most now do.

## The reversal price method, demonstrated live

- Turn on the **Spot** button on the option chain. The display switches from last traded prices to reversal prices.
- The call side, on the left, carries resistance data. The put side, on the right, carries support data.
- The rule stated plainly: the market falls from a reversal price on the call side, and rises from a reversal price on the put side. Each side gives a ladder of levels, so a rally has a designated place to turn down and a decline has a designated place to turn up.
- Decide by proximity. Whichever reversal price is closest to current price, trade away from it towards the far one.
- Worked example on randomly chosen historical data, 13 March, 12:00 PM: price at 23,363, with a call-side reversal at 23,412 and a put-side reversal near 23,363. Price was closer to the lower level, so the class bought with a target of 23,412.
- That trade lost. So did the next one. On the third attempt the class sold from 23,363 and it worked. The speaker leaves the two losses in rather than editing them out.
- A second example: with spot at 25,734, resistance at about 25,738 and support around 25,690; above 25,738 the next downward reversal sits at 25,787, and at 25,639 price should turn up.
- He states the reversal prices are calculated from Option Greeks, and that this is only the most basic level of the method.

## What the video tells you to do

- Ignore the spot-versus-futures-versus-options distinction while learning. Track one price.
- Before entering, identify the nearest turning level above and below current price. Ask which will be reached first, not which way the market will go.
- Switch on the Spot button to see reversal prices instead of LTPs.
- Read resistance from the call side and support from the put side.
- Enter in the direction of the level that is further away from current price.
- Practise on random historical dates and times, chosen by someone else, so you cannot know the outcome in advance.
- Treat any indicator as guidance on whether to buy or sell first, and only then learn its finer conditions.
