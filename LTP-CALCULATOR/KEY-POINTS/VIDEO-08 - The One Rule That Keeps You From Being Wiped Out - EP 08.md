## In one line

Gives the single tie-breaking rule for grading a support or resistance that is built on volume *and* open interest together: if either challenger is moving towards the imaginary line, treat the level as weak.

## The idea behind the rule

- The previous episode graded levels made of one factor. Here both volume and open interest sit at the level, so they can disagree.
- The question asked of each one is directional: is its yellow challenger forming **towards** the imaginary line, or **away** from it?
- A challenger forming towards the line is pressing in on the market; one forming away from it is retreating.

## The rule itself

- If **either** the volume or the open interest has its challenger coming towards the imaginary line, the level is weak, and you follow that one.
- For the level to count as strong, **both** must be clean or both moving away. One moving away is not enough.
- Applied to the two sides:
  - A weak support is weak towards top.
  - A weak resistance is weak towards bottom.
- Example: Nifty support at 19,500, built on both volume and open interest. The open interest challenger is moving away, but the volume challenger is moving towards the line — so the volume is believed and the support is graded weak towards top.

## Examples worked through

- **Indiabulls Housing Finance**: both support and resistance are open-interest-only with no yellow on either — both strong.
- **SBI Life**: volume-only support with no challenger moving out, so strong; volume-only resistance whose challenger sits at a higher strike, so weak towards top.
- A case where the biggest volume sits at 1,600 but in the money, and is therefore not counted as the support — 1,500 is used instead.
- **Tata Power**: volume and open interest both strong. The highlight was temporarily missing because the stock price was sitting exactly on the 257.5 strike, which stops the tool calculating. Checking the historical data at 3:25 PM, once the price had moved to 257.25, restored the highlight.

## What the video tells you to do

- On any level made of both factors, check the direction of each challenger before grading it, and let the one pointing at the imaginary line decide.
- If a highlight is missing, check whether the price is sitting exactly on a strike price, and re-check a minute or two either side in the historical data.
- Pull the full list of stocks currently on support or resistance and grade them repeatedly until the reading becomes automatic.
