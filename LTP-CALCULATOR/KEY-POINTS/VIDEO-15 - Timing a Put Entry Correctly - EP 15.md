## Where the rules live

## What "weak towards the top" means

- The resistance volume is trying to move up a strike — for example from 19,600 towards 19,700. On screen it appears as a yellow box on that side.
- Weak towards the top is weakness only. Shifting is a different event: the highest volume actually moves to the new strike. Keep the two apart.
- Resistance is read from volume, not from open interest. Open interest can be weak towards the bottom at the same time without changing the reading.

## The 13 September example

- At 9:20 am support was weak towards the bottom, so the market fell an extra 10–15 points and bottomed near 19,947.50.
- By 9:45 the support had turned strong on its own. With both sides strong, extension of support was 19,965 and extension of resistance about 20,014. The morning high of 20,006 was made against that resistance extension.
- With both sides strong, the first touch of a level gives the best reversal. From the second touch onwards the odds shift towards the market running on to the next divergence — 20,050's divergence at 20,062 on the upside, 19,916 on the downside.
- By 12:43 pm the market was circling the 20,065 divergence. By 1:45 pm the resistance had turned weak towards the top.

## The rule to write down

- When resistance is weak towards the top, the market can move at most to one strike price below the strike the weakness reaches — its divergence, 20,050 in this example. That is the day's ceiling while the condition holds.
- Above that level the market may poke out 10–25 points but comes back and hits the same value repeatedly.
- If the entry value is not offered, use extension of support plus one strike: click the put side of the next higher strike (20,050) to get a usable value around 20,019. If the market then falls below the support extension, the position is averaged rather than abandoned.
- The day's whole range in this scenario runs from the extension of support up to the weak-towards-top level minus one strike.

## What the video tells you to do

- In this setup the supported trade is a call buy at the extension of support, not a put.
- If you still take the put near the ceiling, treat it as a trade against Chart of Accuracy 1.0 and watch the percentage on the weak-towards-top side. If it climbs towards 90, 95, 97, the volume is shifting — exit and book the loss.
- A shift adds a new extension of resistance a strike higher and roughly another 100 points of upside, which is the specific risk in that put.
- Safer alternative: if you bought from the bottom, book the profit at the ceiling instead of expecting more upside.
