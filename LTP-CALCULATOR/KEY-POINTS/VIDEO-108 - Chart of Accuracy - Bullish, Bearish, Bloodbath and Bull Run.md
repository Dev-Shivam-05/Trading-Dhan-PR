## The five states of a level

Each level — resistance or support — is classified into one of five conditions, and each carries a directional pressure:

| State | What happened | Pressure |
|---|---|---|
| Stable Strong | Opened at a strike and stayed there all session | Neutral |
| WTT to Strong | Tried to move to a higher strike, then abandoned the attempt and returned | Bearish |
| WTB to Strong | Tried to move to a lower strike, then abandoned it and returned | Bullish |
| Shifted Bottom to Top | Completed the move to a higher strike and became strong there | Bullish |
| Shifted Top to Bottom | Completed the move to a lower strike and became strong there | Bearish |

- The logic: a level that attempts a move and then gives it up produces pressure in the *opposite* direction. A level that completes the move produces pressure in the direction it went.
- "Weak towards" is measured by percentage. A neighbouring strike crossing about 75% is highlighted yellow and marks the attempted shift. Below 75% the level is treated as strong.
- The same five states apply to support and to resistance independently.

## How the scenarios are built

- The scenario label at the top of the screen — bull run, blood bath, both side risky COA 8, and so on — is the combination of the support's pressure and the resistance's pressure.
- Chart of Accuracy 1.0 defines nine such scenarios. The speaker says the underlying combinations run into thousands, all of which collapse into those nine.
- His advice to viewers who ask him to enumerate every scenario: don't. **Read the pressure on each side rather than memorising the outcomes.** The pressure is what the label is derived from, and it updates continuously through the session.
- The speaker describes developing the framework manually over three to four years before training software to produce the label automatically.
- Simple application: if resistance is strong and support is generating bullish pressure, buy calls only that day. If support is generating bearish pressure, buy puts only.

## Trading a blood bath

- In the blood bath example, AI LTP drew only resistance lines — R Moderate, R Risky and the Max Pain stop level — with no support lines at all. No support lines means no call trades that day.
- Where you enter decides your risk. Selling at the current price, far from Max Pain, would have meant a stop loss of roughly 1,170 points. Waiting for price to come within a few points of Max Pain would have meant a stop of five points.
- **The trade-off is stated plainly:** the tighter the stop, the safer the trade, the rarer it is, and the lower the total return. A setup that only triggers once in 10 to 25 days cannot produce much. A loose stop triggers constantly but loses more often.
- The mindset he asks for: think about how often a particular trade will actually be executed, not how many points it might make. Points come later.

## The divergence scalping method

- Turn on **Set D** and select **D1** to draw the divergence lines. These are dotted and they move as the market moves.
- Each time price touches a divergence line, take the trade with an **equal stop loss and target** — 10 and 10, 15 and 15, 20 and 20.
- **Keep the same size all day.** If you start the morning with 10 points, stay on 10 points; do not switch to 25 points mid-session.
- **Maximum 25 points**, and never more than the gap between two strike prices. Stretching past that, he says, turns seven, eight or nine trades out of ten into losses, because the move simply is not there.
- Do not re-trade a line that has already given its result.
- When the scenario flips — for example from both side risky to bull run — stop taking trades on the side the scenario no longer supports and wait for a line on the other side.
- In the replay he takes a put at a divergence, exits 10 points later at target, then the scenario turns to bull run, so he waits for the call-side line and takes a long with a 10-point stop and a 10-point target, which also pays.
- He calls this diversion-to-diversion trading, and treats it as scalping.
- For a larger trade than this, he directs you back to the AI LTP lines rather than the divergences.

## What the video tells you to do

- Read the pressure label on support and on resistance before reading the scenario name.
- Take trades only on the side the scenario allows; if the lines for one side are absent, that direction is not available.
- Decide your stop loss distance by choosing where you enter relative to Max Pain, and accept the frequency that comes with it.
- Fix one point value for the day, use a 1:1 stop and target, and never exceed 25 points or the strike gap.
- Stop trading a side as soon as the scenario changes against it.
- Backtest the method on random past dates before using it live.
