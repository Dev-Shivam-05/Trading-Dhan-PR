## The setup and why it traps people

- Scenario 2 is support strong plus resistance weak towards bottom (shown in yellow on the LTP Calculator). It is a bearish structure.
- The trap: a trader sees strong support, assumes the market must reverse there, and buys Calls. The market breaks the support instead.
- The Put entry comes from the weak-towards-bottom resistance, not from the support.

## How far the market falls, and how much you risk

- The depth of the fall does not depend on how many strike prices the yellow weak-towards-bottom band sits below resistance. In every case the target is the same: one strike below the extension of support, which the speaker writes as EOS-1.
- What the distance does change is how far the market rises before falling, and therefore your risk.
- Example with resistance at 19,950: if the weak band is one strike lower, selling at its divergence risks about 50 points up to the extension of resistance. At 19,900 the risk becomes about 100 points; at 19,850 about 150 points.
- So the nearer the weak-towards-bottom band is to resistance, the safer the Put trade. The further away it is, the riskier.

## A scenario can be cancelled mid-day

- The example is the 23 October session. At 9:18–9:21 the chain showed resistance weak towards top with strong support — scenario 3, bullish.
- If the yellow band moves to a different strike price, the old scenario is cut and a new one begins. A move from bottom to top is bullish shifting; a move from top to bottom is bearish.
- Around 9:41 the resistance shifted downward — from 19,600 to the divergence of 19,550 and then to 19,500. That reversed the morning's bullish read into a bearish one.
- The speaker stresses that this can happen inside two minutes, which is why he watches the chain refresh rather than a fixed morning view.

## Trading the session that followed

- With the weak band at 19,500, the Call-side value of that strike read 19,544 while the market traded at 19,536, so that was the sell point.
- A safer, later entry was the divergence of 19,550, around 19,596.
- Targets: the extension of support at about 19,497, then EOS-1 at about 19,449–19,450. The market fell roughly 100 points and stopped at the lower line.
- By 10:23 the resistance shifted a second time. Two bearish shifts in a row — 19,600 to 19,550, then 19,550 to 19,500 — is what the speaker calls double shifting, and it means the fall will not stop at EOS-1.
- In that case he extends targets: EOS-2 at 19,400 with a value of about 19,402, and then the divergence of 19,350 at about 19,359.
- His counter-example: if the resistance had jumped from 19,600 straight to a strong 19,500 without pausing at 19,550, there would have been no double shift and the market would have reversed from the extension of support instead.

## What the video tells you to do

- In this scenario, take the Put entry at the value of the weak-towards-bottom strike, not at the support.
- Measure your risk before entering as the distance from that entry up to the extension of resistance.
- If you want a safer fill, wait for the divergence of the strike one step above instead of selling at the first value.
- Stop counting on Call buys once double shifting appears; use the extensions of support only as profit targets.
- Re-read the chain through the session. If the yellow band moves, treat the earlier scenario as cancelled.
