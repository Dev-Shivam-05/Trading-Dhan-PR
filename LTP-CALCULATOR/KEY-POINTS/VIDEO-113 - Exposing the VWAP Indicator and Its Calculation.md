## The calculation

- VWAP is the sum of (price multiplied by volume) at every tick, divided by the total volume.
- Worked example from the video: three price points around 22,450, 22,520 and 22,380 with their traded volumes. Multiplying each by its volume, summing, and dividing by total volume gives a VWAP near 22,429 while price sits around 22,450.
- The same calculation repeats every tick, which is what draws the line.
- The image he uses: price is a pendulum swinging up and down, volume is the weight hanging on it, and VWAP is the centre of gravity the swing keeps returning to.

## What the formula rules out

- Volume resets to zero every day and is never carried forward. That eliminates positional trading immediately - a VWAP line means nothing beyond the session it was built in.
- A common worry is that rising volume against falling price unbalances the indicator. It does not, because volume appears in both the numerator and the denominator.
- VWAP works only in a range-bound market, and only when volume is spread evenly through the session. A stock that trades heavily for five minutes and then not at all for fifteen gives a useless line.

## When it fails

- Uneven volume days. Sensex the day after expiry, or any index on a news or RBI-policy day, distributes volume in bursts and breaks the line.
- Very heavy volume early in the session. The denominator becomes so large that VWAP effectively freezes in place, stays far below price, and may take most of the day to converge.
- Sudden large moves. VWAP is built from the last completed one- or five-minute interval, so a fast jump or a large block trade leaves the line behind.

## The misuse the video is aimed at

- Most people treat a touch of VWAP as a buy signal. It is not a signal of anything; it is a mean, the market's fair value.
- Price returning to VWAP means only that price has come back to fair value. It can then continue straight through and spend time below it.
- If you use VWAP, use it to judge fair value and check support and resistance separately for the actual entry.

## What the video tells you to do

- Never apply VWAP to positional trades.
- Before using any indicator, work out its formula and identify the conditions under which the formula breaks.
- Skip VWAP on event days and on instruments with lumpy volume.
