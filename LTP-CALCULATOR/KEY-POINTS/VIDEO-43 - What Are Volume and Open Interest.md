# What Are Volume and Open Interest

**Video 43 of 133** · 24 min · [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=98efa_DisZ0)

## In one line

How volume differs from Open Interest, worked through a running example, and the exact rule the LTP Calculator uses to locate support and resistance from those two columns.

## Volume versus Open Interest

- Only a writer can create Open Interest. Volume is created by writers and buyers alike — every completed transaction adds to it.
- Open Interest can rise or fall during the day. Volume only ever increases.
- Volume resets every day. It is always intraday and never carried forward, even though an options contract itself may run for several days to weekly expiry.
- The "change in OI" column can go negative. That simply means today's Open Interest is lower than yesterday's close. Volume's change never goes negative.

## The worked example

The episode builds one running scenario, step by step:

| Step | Open Interest | Volume |
|---|---|---|
| Writers offer 5,000 lots, nobody buys | 0 | 0 |
| Buyers take 3,000 lots | 3,000 | 3,000 |
| Those buyers sell 2,000 lots | 3,000 | 5,000 |
| Writers buy back 1,000 lots | 2,000 | 6,000 |

- The Open Interest stays at 3,000 when buyers sell among themselves, because the writer's position has not changed.
- Open Interest only falls when the writer closes out.
- An offer that nobody takes is invisible on the screen. Until a buyer transacts, there is no Open Interest and no volume to see.

## Why overtrading works against the trader

- Volume is the one input a buyer controls. Every extra round trip adds to it.
- The pattern described: a green candle prompts a Call buy, a red candle prompts a Put, and the trader flips back and forth all session.
- That flow of buying builds volume at a strike, which attracts writers, which builds Open Interest, which forms resistance on the Call side. The trader's own activity helps create the level that blocks the move he wants.
- The stated conclusion: pick one trade instead of many, and watch the crowd that is doing multiple trades rather than joining it.

## The rule for finding support and resistance

Both start from the pair of strikes at the imaginary line and move in the direction where the LTP is falling (out of the money).

- **Support** — on the Put side, start from the **higher** strike of the pair and read downwards.
- **Resistance** — on the Call side, start from the **lower** strike of the pair and read upwards.
- In that direction, find the strike showing the highest volume and the strike showing the highest Open Interest, each marked as 100 percent.
- If they sit at different strikes, the one **closer to the imaginary line** is the level. If both fall on the same strike, that strike is support (or resistance) of both volume and Open Interest.
- Examples worked on screen: Nifty support at 21900 and resistance at 22000, both on volume; Bank Nifty resistance at 46000 and support at 45800; a stock with support at 165 and resistance at 170.
- The LTP Calculator prints "current range", "resistance" and "support" at the top of the screen, so the reading can be checked against the tool.

## What the video tells you to do

- Read Open Interest and volume as separate things: OI for open positions, volume for activity.
- Locate the imaginary line pair first, then move outward in the falling-LTP direction to find the level.
- When two candidate strikes compete, take the one nearer the imaginary line.
- Cut the number of trades per day rather than trying to catch every candle.
