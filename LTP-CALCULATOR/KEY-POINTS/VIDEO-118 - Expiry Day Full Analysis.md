## Reading the weekly range

- The W button on the app draws the weekly range lines - RL1, RL2, RL3 above and SL1, SL2, SL3 below - derived from standard deviation.
- On expiry day, note which two lines the market sits between and re-check every hour from about 10 am. It may be between two resistance lines, two support lines, or one of each.
- In the session's example the current week's band ran from RL1 at 23,108 down to SL1 at 21,554 - a range of about 1,554 points, which the speaker calls unusually wide.
- The reversal odds attached to these lines: roughly 65 times out of 100 the market turns at L1, 95 out of 100 at L2, and 99 out of 100 at L3.
- That is the basis for the calls he references - the Nifty bottom during the India-Pakistan episode, when a 1,000-point gap-down reached L3 and L1 had already been touched twice; a top at L3 a month earlier; and a Budget Day level of 24,600 at L2.

## Estimating next week's range from today's chain

- On expiry day, open the next expiry's option chain rather than the one about to settle.
- Take the at-the-money strike and add the call's last traded price to the put's. In the example, the 23,000 strike gave about 700 points combined.
- Discount that figure for the time of day: about 10% if read before noon, 7 to 8% after 12, about 5% after 2 pm. 700 points became roughly 650.
- Add and subtract that number from spot to get next week's RL1 and SL1 - here 23,650 above and 22,350 below, a full range of 1,300 points against this week's 1,554.
- Then compare: is next week's RL1 higher or lower than this week's, and the same for SL1?

## The decision rule

- If next week's RL1 comes out higher than the current one, do not take a bearish cheap-option punt this expiry.
- If next week's SL1 comes out higher than the current one and price is sitting near support, the bullish version of that trade is the one worth taking.
- The reasoning is that the market has to settle inside its own range, so a rising projected range removes the downside case.
- Distance matters too. If the opposite line is 400 to 500 points away, the punt has room to work. At 1,500 points away it does not.

## Hero-zero trades and a better alternative

- A hero-zero trade means buying a Rs 5 or Rs 10 premium option on expiry hoping it multiplies. The speakers state plainly that eight times out of ten it goes to zero.
- Their suggested substitute: when the market is near a weekly-range level after 12 noon and has to close at that range, take the same view in futures instead. The move is captured without the near-certain total loss.
- Expiry days tend to resolve one of two ways: the market dies inside the range, or it produces one sharp move of a hundred points or more right at the end.

## Reading the writers' remaining money in the last hour

- Look at the time-value column on the in-the-money side of the chain, which the app shades grey. Intrinsic value is irrelevant on the day contracts expire.
- A writer will sell wherever the most money is still lying, then the next-best strike, working outwards.
- Where time value goes to near zero or negative, no writer will show up because brokerage would eat the whole premium. That strike marks a level the market will not be seen past in the remaining time.
- In the example the put-side time value turned negative around 23,150, so no put writer would arrive there and the market was unlikely to trade above it. On the call side money was still available five or six strikes out, so call writers could keep pressing the market down.
- The speakers state the limitation firmly: this reading applies only on expiry day, only in the last hour, and only on the grey side of the chain. It says nothing about a Tuesday or a Wednesday.

## What the video tells you to do

- On expiry day, check the weekly range lines hourly rather than once.
- Compute next week's range from the next expiry's ATM straddle before deciding on any expiry-day punt.
- Prefer futures over near-worthless options when you want to express an expiry-day view.
- In the final hour, use the time-value column to find where writers will and will not go, and treat that as the day's boundary.
