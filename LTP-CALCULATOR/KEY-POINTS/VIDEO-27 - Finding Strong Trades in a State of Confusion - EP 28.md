## In one line

How to identify the speaker's "state of confusion" — one side of the chain strong, the other unable to settle on a level — and why a confused support side means a bearish day whichever way the weakness points.

## Spotting it early

- The example is Nifty on 7 November, the first of three consecutive days used across three episodes.
- Resistance was strong from the open near 19,400, but support could not be pinned down: 19,400, 19,350 and 19,300 were all competing.
- The first clue is volume developing at three strikes at the same time — here roughly 2,34,000, 96,000 and 1,89,000, all still growing at 9:19 and 9:29.
- Three developing strikes show up early in the morning. When only two are involved you usually cannot tell until the afternoon.

## The two ways it is confirmed

- First form: the weakness percentage on the undecided side holds at the same level for a full hour without shifting. On this day it sat near 80 from 9:33 through 9:47 and was still only 85–86 past 10:25.
- Second form: the shift does complete inside the hour but the level still does not become strong — for instance weak towards top stuck at 98 or 99. In that case wait one more hour, and if it is still unresolved declare a state of confusion. So the decision takes up to two hours.
- Throughout, the other side must remain strong. Here resistance stayed strong all morning.

## What it means for direction

- If the confusion is on the support side, the market will be bearish. This holds whether that support reads weak towards bottom or weak towards top.
- That is the important reversal: in normal conditions a support weak towards top is bullish. Inside a state of confusion it is not.
- If the confusion is on the resistance side instead, the market will be bullish.

## What the video tells you to do

- Watch for three strikes building volume together in the first fifteen minutes as the early warning of a confused session.
- Do not trade until the confusion is confirmed by the one-hour, or two-hour, percentage test.
- Once confirmed on the support side, sell rallies and treat the divergence below the lowest support candidate as the day's floor.
- Do not overtrade the wait. Ignore 10 to 25 point swings and only average a position when price actually reaches the level you nominated in advance.
- Ignore a blue level appearing at a lower strike mid-fall; the original support strike is still the one that defines the target.
