## In one line

A single anti-overtrading rule: read two reversal values off the option chain, buy the call only at the lower one and the put only at the upper one, and take no trade at all in between — whatever strategy you otherwise use.

## The problem being described

- The urge to buy a call or a put arrives constantly while watching candles, and the result is call, put, call, put through the day.
- The trades in the middle of the range go nowhere. Every 10 or 15 points a new position is opened, then averaged, then reversed.
- The speaker states the rule works regardless of method — candlesticks, indicators, any strategy. Only the entry price changes.

## How the two values are read

- Find the two strike prices sitting either side of the imaginary line. The lower of the two is the call-side strike; the higher is the put-side strike.
- Click the **volume** on the call side of the lower strike. Either a value appears or the word "breakout" does. If it is a breakout, click the next higher strike instead. The value that appears is the put-buying level.
- Click the volume on the put side of the higher strike. Either a value appears or the word "breakdown" does. If it is a breakdown, click the next lower strike. That value is the call-buying level.
- The result is two prices, one above and one below where the market is now. Nothing is done between them.

## What the video tells you to do

- Wait for one of the two values. Do not enter anywhere in between, however strong the candle looks.
- Trade the call only at the lower value and the put only at the upper value.
- If you end up on the wrong side, use the fact that the market returns to these values repeatedly to exit near cost rather than holding.
- Back-test the method yourself on random dates using the historical data section of the app.
