## The two numbers being used

- **Open interest** is the writer's position that is still open — sold but not yet bought back.
- **Volume** counts every trade of the day. A buy and a sell are two separate trades, so both add to volume. Ten lots count as ten lots multiplied by the lot size.
- In the tool, the largest volume on each side is marked 100% and the largest open interest on each side is marked 100%. The second-highest is shown as a percentage of it and highlighted in yellow **only when it is large enough to matter that day**. ## Finding the real ATM strike

- A red imaginary line on the chain marks the live spot price. In the example it sits at 25,046-47, between the 25,000 and 25,050 strikes.
- People disagree about which of those two is ATM. The speaker's rule: **the ATM is the strike with the highest time value**, and there can only ever be one.
- The IV/TV tool at the top of the chain splits each side into implied volatility and, separately, intrinsic value and time value. In the example the 25,050 strike carried ₹66 of call-side time value and ₹63 on the put side, the highest on the chain, making 25,050 the ATM.
- The imaginary line itself is drawn using the highest time value, between those two strikes.

## Locating support and resistance on the chain

- **Resistance**: start at the lower of the two strikes at the imaginary line and read outward on the call side. Of the highest open interest and the highest volume, whichever sits **closer to the imaginary line** is the resistance.
- **Support**: start at the higher strike and read outward on the put side, towards out-of-the-money. Again, whichever of highest open interest or highest volume is nearer the line is the support.
- The tool prints the result at the top anyway — in the example, resistance 25,000 and support 25,000.

## Reading the status labels

- **Strong** means the level is holding firmly.
- **WTB** = weak towards bottom; **WTT** = weak towards top. As a first approximation WTB implies bearish pressure and WTT bullish pressure.
- The speaker warns this shortcut often reverses. A WTB label can still carry bullish pressure, and a WTT label can carry bearish pressure, depending on how the percentages moved during the day. He says you cannot catch this with the naked eye unless you watch the chain continuously from 9:15.
- What settles it is the transition line printed under the level. In the example, resistance read "WTB to Strong" — that is bullish — and support read "shifted from bottom to top", also bullish. Both sides bullish is why the market was in a bull run at the time.
- **SOC** stands for State of Confusion, shown with a duration and a direction, for example "bearish SOC, 3 hours". Red colouring means the confusion is on the support side.
- Clicking the SOC banner shows when each level last changed. In the example, resistance had been unchanged and strong since 10:00 am; support had shifted at 10:40 am from 25,000 to 24,900. Current time on screen: 12:46.

## The 75% threshold

- The tool flags caution when open interest or volume around a level reaches **75% or more** of the highest value on that side, because it could climb towards 100% and flip the level.
- Below 75% it stays quiet. In the live example, the figure dropped from 75% to 74.99%, the bearish pressure warning disappeared immediately, and the bull-run reading resumed.

## What the video tells you to do

- Identify the ATM by time value, not by nearest strike, before reading anything else on the chain.
- Read outwards from the imaginary line to find which strike is support and which is resistance.
- Do not act on the WTT/WTB label alone. Check the transition text and the SOC state to see which way the pressure is actually running.
- Treat a level whose surrounding volume or open interest has crossed 75% as unreliable.
