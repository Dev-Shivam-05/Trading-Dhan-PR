A variant of the "both sides strong" scenario: when support and resistance only turn strong after the open, the day behaves differently from the textbook case, and this video works through one such session on Nifty.

## Why the history of a level matters

- Two levels that look identical in a snapshot can behave completely differently depending on how they got there.

## Reading the 16 February 2024 open

- First tick at 9:15: resistance at 22,000 carrying both Open Interest and volume; support also at 22,000 but carrying volume only, and weak towards bottom.
- The resistance was not weak towards bottom even though it looked it. The definition given: a resistance is weak towards bottom only if it sits below the smaller strike of the imaginary-line pair. With price near 21,998 that would require the market to have traded under 21,950.
- The speaker checks the candle chart and confirms Nifty never traded below about 21,970 that day, so the condition failed and the resistance counted as strong.
- As the first minutes passed, the support's weak-towards-bottom percentage deepened - bearish pressure building, implying a reversal down from the extension of resistance.
- By 9:20 both levels read strong. This is scenario one of Chart of Accuracy 1.0, but arrived at late rather than present from the open.

## Why late strength changes the day

- Treat each level as a crowd pushing price. Equal pressure on both sides means price simply rotates between them.
- Here the support first dropped, then pushed back up, so support-side pressure exceeded resistance-side pressure. The resistance is weaker than its label suggests.
- Consequence: price frequently runs past the extension of resistance to the next line up, the extension of resistance plus one.
- The downside stayed capped because the support shifted upward, so price would not fall far.
- The result that day was a 50-point band, roughly 22,001 at the bottom and about 22,051 at the top, and price stayed in it until the 2:30 pm close.

## How to trade this variant

- **Safe put entry:** wait for the extension of resistance plus one. Selling puts at the plain extension of resistance is what gets a safe trader stuck in this variant.
- **Risky put entry:** enter at the extension of resistance, then average at the plus-one line if price continues up, and book at cost or in profit when price returns.
- **Calls:** buying at the extension of support is available but risky, because price can reverse before reaching it. The exception is the first touch of the day - a level that has not been tested yet will reverse on that first hit.
- Inside a 50-point band the video's view is that opportunities are plentiful and repeated, and that trading the range is more likely to keep you flat than to lose money.

## What the video tells you to do

- Replay the day from 9:15 in historical data before deciding which scenario you are in. Do not classify a level from a single snapshot.
- Check whether a level was strong at the open or only became strong later. If it became strong later, shift the entry out to the plus-one line.
- Check the candle low against the imaginary-line strike before calling anything weak towards bottom.
- Wait roughly five minutes after 9:15 before acting, since the classification can change in the first minutes.
