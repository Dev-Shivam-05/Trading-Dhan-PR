## In one line

Presents the market as a tug of war over the imaginary line, driven by the fact that every out-of-the-money premium is trying to decay to zero by expiry — call-side premiums pulling the market down and put-side premiums pulling it up.

## Why support and resistance exist at all

- The speaker frames support and resistance as market gravity. Gravity is what makes something directional and controlled; without it the thing simply drifts, like an object in space.
- Buyers and sellers create that gravity through demand and supply at particular strike prices, which is what holds the market inside a range.
- He says this idea is what convinced him the market could be calculated, and is the origin of the LTP Calculator.

## The tug of war

- Picture the imaginary line as a heavy pole. The call side pushes it one way, the put side pushes it the other.
- Every option premium has one wish: to be zero at 3:30 PM on Thursday's expiry.
- Call-side premiums are extinguished by a falling market, so the call side's pressure is downward — towards the bottom of the chain.
- Put-side premiums are extinguished by a rising market, so the put side's pressure is upward.
- The further the market travels in either direction, the more premiums on that side get wiped out, which is why the push keeps going.
- This is the reasoning behind the previous episode's branch analogy: buying calls generates volume that adds to the downward pressure.
- *Why* a premium decays to zero is deferred to a later chapter on intrinsic and extrinsic value.

## The worked example — Bank Nifty, 24 August expiry

- At 10:15 AM the imaginary line sat between the 44,900 and 45,000 strikes.
- By the 3:30 PM close it had moved down to sit between 44,400 and 44,500.
- The 44,500 call premium, about ₹58 in the morning, finished at zero. Call premiums above the closing level all collapsed to five and ten paise.
- On the put side the 44,900 premium was about ₹105 in the morning and rose sharply by the close as the market fell.
- The speaker asks viewers to decide in the comments which side won that day's tug of war.

## What the video tells you to do

- Open the historical-data view in the LTP Calculator, pick a past expiry day, and compare the imaginary line's position in the morning with its position at 3:30 PM.
- Check which premiums went to zero and which gained, and use that to judge which side was pushing harder.
