The first of the detailed Chart of Accuracy 1.0 scenarios - what to do when both support and resistance are strong - and how to draw the four lines that mark the safe and risky reversal points.

## Why the speaker says direction matters more than entries

- Small losses are routine and survivable. The damage comes from one trade taken against the market's direction.
- The pattern he describes: a small loss becomes two days of profit, then five days, then the position grows so large the trader cannot bring himself to square it off, and a month's earnings disappear in one trade.
- His rule is therefore not about better entries but about never taking a position against the Chart of Accuracy reading, in Nifty, Bank Nifty, midcaps or stocks.

## Scenario one: both sides strong

- The setup is strong resistance and strong support. Resistance here was carrying both volume and Open Interest; support was carrying volume.
- Important condition: the level must have been strong from the market open and must not have shifted. If it has shifted during the day, this scenario does not apply.
- In this configuration reversals happen at the extension of resistance and the extension of support, not at the levels themselves.
- The example is Nifty on the morning of 7 February, with the key strike at 22,000.

## The four lines

To get each line, click the volume on the relevant strike and read the value it returns.

1. **Extension of resistance** - click the call-side volume of the 22,000 strike. This returned about 22,028. Upper line.
2. **Extension of support** - the matching put-side reading, about 21,959. Lower line.
3. **OR+1** - one strike price above the resistance, i.e. the 22,050 strike. Its value came out near 22,058. Second upper line.
4. **OS-1** - one strike price below the support, i.e. the 21,950 strike. Its value came out near 21,909. Second lower line.

The same notation extends further out: OR+2 is two strikes above the resistance, OS-2 two strikes below the support.

## How the lines are used

- **First hit is safe.** The first time price reaches the extension of resistance or the extension of support, the reversal trade can be taken from that line on either side.
- **Second hit is risky.** By then the first hit has weakened the level, more time has passed - the first touch may have been at 9:20-9:30 and the second at 10:30 - and volume, Open Interest and overall pressure have built up.
- On a second hit a risky trader can still enter at the extension line and, if price runs against him, average at the OR+1 or OS-1 line.
- A safe trader skips the extension line entirely on the second hit and waits for OR+1 (for a put) or OS-1 (for a call). Which of the two you use depends purely on how much risk you are willing to carry.

## What the video tells you to do

- Confirm the levels were strong from the open and have not shifted before applying this scenario at all.
- Draw all four lines on the chart before the first hit, not after it.
- Take the reversal on the first touch of an extension; on any later touch, either wait for the plus-one or minus-one line or plan the averaging level in advance.
