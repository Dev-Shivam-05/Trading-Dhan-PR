## What scalping demands

- Scalping means taking the same setup repeatedly inside one session, with targets and stops of roughly five to ten points each time.
- It requires sustained concentration and very high screen time, so the trading position needs to be somewhere free of interruption.
- Slippage is the recurring problem. The terminal's slippage percentage matters more here than in any other style.

## Terminal features the video highlights

- **Scalper mode** shows two charts side by side with buy and sell buttons on the charts themselves.
- **Set defaults** stores a trade quantity per instrument in advance — for example one lot (75) in Nifty, two lots in Sensex, three in Bank Nifty — so the order size never has to be typed.
- Auto stop loss and target can be preset in points, with a trailing stop option. The example sets both to 25 points.
- **One-click buy/sell** then fires the order with the stored quantity, stop loss and target in a single press.
- Selecting Nifty auto-loads the at-the-money call; one click switches to the put, and the strike can be changed in a click.
- Layouts allow index spot, call chart, put chart and option chain on one screen, resizable without the panes disturbing each other, so multiple monitors are not needed.
- A **notification sound switch** plays an audible alert on execution, target or stop loss, so attention is pulled back to the screen.
- A **kill switch** in the bottom left disables trading in a chosen segment for the rest of the day. The video frames this as a psychological tool for the moment a scalper develops a one-directional bias and keeps re-entering against the market.
- An **OI resistance / OI support indicator** is added from the FX menu and plots the highest call and put open interest directly on the spot chart, removing the need to open the option chain. In the example it drew resistance at 26,000 and support at 25,700.

## Brokerage as the scalper's raw material cost

- Brokerage, not hardware, is the scalper's largest cost, because the trade count is high.
- The offer described: zero brokerage for the first 30 days after the FAO segment is activated on a new Sahi account, then ₹10 per order. Taxes still apply during the free period.
- Against a ₹20 per order benchmark this halves the cost per trade, which the video frames as smaller losses on losing trades and larger net profit on winners.

## What the video tells you to do

- Preset trade quantity, stop loss and target per instrument before the session so orders need no typing during it.
- Build one layout with index spot, call and put charts and the option chain together rather than switching screens.
- Turn on the execution sound alert.
- Use the kill switch the moment you notice yourself repeating the same directional trade out of bias.
- Add the OI support and resistance indicator to the spot chart instead of reading the option chain separately.
