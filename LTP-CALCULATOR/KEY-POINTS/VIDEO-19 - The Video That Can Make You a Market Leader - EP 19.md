## In one line

Strike price selection done arithmetically: tabulate what each strike gains on a 50-point rise against what it loses on a 50-point fall, and pick the strike with the best gap between the two.

## The shortcut for estimating premiums

- Nifty strikes are 50 points apart. A 50-point move in the index pushes a strike's premium to roughly whatever the adjacent strike's premium is right now.
- Verified with the OC calculate box: the 19,550 call at ₹99 becomes about ₹125–126 on a 50-point rise, which is what the 19,500 call is trading at, and about ₹75 on a 50-point fall — the premium of the strike below.
- The same works on the put side: a ₹103 put becomes about ₹128–129 on a 50-point fall, and roughly ₹80 on a 50-point rise.
- So no calculation is needed to estimate a target. Read down or up the chain in steps of the strike gap. The same logic works in Bank Nifty and in stocks, with their own strike spacing.

## The table that answers the question

- For each strike, record the premium now, the premium after a 50-point rise, the resulting profit, the premium after a 50-point fall, and the resulting loss.
- Moving from far out of the money towards deep in the money, the profit on the same 50-point move runs roughly: 13, 16, 20, 25, 27, 31, 38, 37, 38.
- The instinct is to take the strike showing 38. But the losses have to be read alongside: the deepest in-the-money strikes lose about 37–38 on the same 50-point move against you.
- The best pairing in this example was the **19,450 call: about +38 on a 50-point rise against about −31 on a 50-point fall**. Most money for the movement, least risk for the same movement.
- Out-of-the-money strikes give both a small gain and a small loss — the movement simply is not paid for.

## The general rule

- The best strike is usually the second one in from the imaginary line on the in-the-money side, occasionally the third. That holds on both the call and the put side.
- After doing this by hand eight to twenty-five times, the IVTV tab replaces the arithmetic: reading intrinsic value against time value gives the same answer at a glance.

## What the video tells you to do

- Build the profit-and-loss table for a one-strike move before choosing where to trade, not after.
- Choose on the profit-to-loss gap, never on the largest profit alone.
- Use the calculate box for the exact entry price, and the adjacent-strike shortcut for a rough target. Place the limit order and leave it — the target arrives on its own.
