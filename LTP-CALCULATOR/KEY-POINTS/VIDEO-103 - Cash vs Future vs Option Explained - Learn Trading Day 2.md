## Spot versus futures

- The Nifty index itself cannot be bought — it is only a number derived from 50 stocks. Exposure is taken through Nifty futures, lot size 75.
- **Spot:** you can buy and hold indefinitely; shares are delivered into your demat. You can also sell before buying, but then you must buy back the same day — that is intraday.
- **Futures:** a contract between two parties with no share delivery, in fixed lot sizes decided by the exchange. Three monthly contracts trade at once, and each expires on the last Tuesday of its month, so the position must be closed by then.
- The futures price is the market's view of the price on the expiry date, not today's price. The farmer analogy: a crop is sold on day five for delivery on day ninety, and both sides carry the risk of what the weather does in between.
- **LTP** stands for Last Traded Price, which is where the LTP Calculator's name comes from.

## Short selling, explained with the pen seller

- A salesman who pays the shop owner ₹1,000 upfront owns his pens and can take unsold stock back whenever he likes. That is a normal buy-and-hold.
- A salesman given the pens on credit by the shop assistant must return the unsold pens and the cash before 3:30, because the assistant has to face the owner. That is a short sale.
- The market equivalent: if you sell shares you do not own, someone else bought them and needs them in their demat, so you must buy them back before the close of the same day.

## Cash-futures arbitrage

- Structure: buy the shares in spot and sell the same quantity — one full lot — in futures, then hold both.
- The logic is that spot and futures must converge by expiry, because the future eventually becomes the present. Whatever the stock does in between, the gap you locked in is yours.
- Path in the tool: menu → Reports → LTP Arbitrage Stocks, which filters the list by the size of the spot-futures gap.
- Live example: SBI Card with spot at 928 against futures at 937, a gap of about ₹10 with only two days to expiry — roughly ₹7,100 on a deployment of about ₹8.7 lakh. Smaller gaps were shown in RBL Bank (about ₹2) and PNB Housing (about ₹6).
- The speaker notes the SBI Card gap was unusual and probably caused by a large trade hitting the market away from the prevailing price.
- Limitations he states: the gaps are usually tiny, they cannot be found by manual searching, and the strategy needs substantial capital because both legs must be funded.

## What the video tells you to do

- Set a fixed monthly amount, however small, and keep adding shares across different sectors rather than concentrating.
- Look at the products around you and work backwards to the listed companies that supply them.
- Before taking a futures position, check which expiry you are buying, because you cannot hold it past that date.
- If you sell in cash without owning the shares, square it off the same day.
- Check Gift Nifty's overnight level against the previous Nifty close to anticipate the open.
- For arbitrage, sell exactly one futures lot against the identical share count in cash, and exit when the two prices meet.
