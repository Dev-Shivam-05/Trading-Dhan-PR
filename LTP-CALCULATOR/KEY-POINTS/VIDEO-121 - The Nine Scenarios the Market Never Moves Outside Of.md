# The Nine Scenarios the Market Never Moves Outside Of

## In one line

A live classroom exercise that shows how to list every way support and resistance can move on a given morning, combine those into a grid, and reduce the result to a single verdict: trade bullish, trade bearish, or stay out.

## The morning routine

- The speaker deliberately ignores overnight news, Gift Nifty and US markets. His argument is that news never stops arriving, and that acting on stale news creates recency bias, which stops you reading the market neutrally.
- His stated condition for trading: be neutral. If you have already decided you are bullish or bearish, the market is not yours that day.
- He does not look at price in the first four minutes. The tool tracks the levels; he reads it afterwards.
- On the day shown, Nifty gapped down. Support sat at 22,700 and resistance at 23,000.

## Step one: mark the outer range

- Write down the support strike, then one strike below it: 22,700 and 22,650.
- Write down the resistance strike, then one strike above it: 23,000 and 23,050.
- That pair of outer values is the day's likely range. The speaker says this is the first thing to do every single morning.

## Step two: list the possible moves on each side

- Resistance can do exactly three things: become strong where it is, shift to a new strike and become strong there, or shift and form weak-towards-top at the new strike. He challenges the room to name a fourth and there isn't one.
- On this day, the resistance side had three listed outcomes and the support side had five, because support could also shift down twice and had a 100% open interest reading appearing at 22,500.
- Multiply the two sides: 3 × 5 = 15 possible paths for the day.
- Reading the volume percentages matters. At 23,000 the volume read 100%, the largest; the next largest, about 95%, was at 22,700. A rising percentage at resistance means the level is being reinforced — his image is a ₹100 note that nobody is pulling away while more stones are piled on top of it.

## Step three: label each outcome bullish or bearish, then combine

- Tag each individual outcome as bullish or bearish. Resistance becoming strong at the **lower** strike is bearish; resistance shifting up and strengthening is bullish. Support shifting down then forming weak-towards-top at a higher strike is bullish pressure even though the level itself moved down.
- Then pair each resistance outcome against each support outcome and write the result: bloodbath, bull run, consolidation, or unpredictable.
- Worked results on this day: resistance strong at 22,700 paired against the five support cases produced three bloodbaths and two consolidations, and no bullish outcome at all. Conclusion: if resistance turns strong at 22,700, trade bearish or stand aside.
- If resistance instead turned strong at 23,000, most pairings came out consolidation or unpredictable. Conclusion: leave that market alone entirely.
- Practical shortcut: work from whichever side has fewer possible outcomes. Three resistance cases are easier to enumerate than five support cases.
- He estimates this takes 5 to 10 minutes a morning, and less with practice. The LTP Calculator already automates it under the headline banner (for example "Nifty in Blood Bath").

## The nine scenarios behind it

- Support and resistance each have three states — strong, weak towards top, weak towards bottom — giving nine combinations. The speaker claims every market that has both volume and open interest, Indian equity, gold, commodities or crypto, stays inside those nine.
- The framework is called Chart 1.0, and the speaker states it is his own theory. He also claims the vocabulary itself — weak towards top, weak towards bottom, extension of support, extension of resistance, divergence, end of divergence — was coined by him, and notes other brokers have since adopted the terms.
- How to read the screen: an **upward arrow means bearish**, a downward arrow means bullish. The reason given is that as the market rises, the imaginary level shifts to a higher strike, which moves it downward on the option chain display.
- Green box means that side is tradeable, red box means it is not. Both green means both Calls and Puts are available.
- The scenario summary: both strong, trade either side. Resistance bearish with support strong, Puts only. Resistance bullish with support strong, Calls only. Both bearish, a bloodbath, Puts only. Both bullish, a bull run, Calls only. One bullish and one bearish, take nothing.

## What the video tells you to do

- Every morning, write down the support strike and one below it, and the resistance strike and one above it.
- List every possible move for resistance and for support, multiply the counts, and label each combination.
- Count how many combinations are bullish, bearish or neutral, and trade only the side the count favours.
- Work from the side with fewer possible outcomes to save time.
- When resistance and support point in opposite directions, take no trade.
- Watch whether the percentage at a level is rising or falling. Rising means the level is being reinforced.
- Consolidation days trade between the max gain and max pain strikes, with the stop loss at the level itself.
