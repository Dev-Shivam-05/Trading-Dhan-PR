## The four lines on a stock

- Stocks show only two tabs, Live and AI LTP, and the method here uses Live. Both community and non-community members get Live once they recharge.
- The Live setting plots four static lines that do not move all day: P2 at the top, then P1, then C1, then C2. Red lines are resistance and relate to put buying; green lines are support and relate to call buying.
- Each line prints its own entry, Max Pain and Max Gain, so no option-chain reading is required. In the Apollo example P2 showed entry 6942, stop 6972, target 6917; P1 showed entry 6893, stop 6923, target 6868.

## The filters that kill most stocks

- Bullish risk and bearish risk must be zero or one on the side you want to trade. Two or more means skip that side. Adani stock showed bullish risk two and bearish risk four, so neither side was tradable.
- C1 and P1 are a pair. If one is hit, the other is dead for the day. C2 and P2 pair the same way.
- If the first candle already reached the target or the stop loss of the line price is sitting on, that line is untradable that day.
- The LTP Blast report only runs between 9:25 and 3:30. The stated reason is that the AI needs five minutes after the open to build the lines. The Show Nearest Stock filter then narrows it to stocks currently sitting at a tradable value, and the risk numbers are visible in the report itself.

## The 920 lines on the index

- At 9:21 four fixed lines appear and hold their values for the rest of the day: extension of resistance (UR), UR+1, extension of support (US), and US-1.
- Buy a call when price reaches US. If it falls further to US-1, do not cut — add the same number of lots. If you cannot fund that averaging, skip US and wait for US-1 instead. Puts mirror this at UR and UR+1.
- To pick the option, find the index level on the opposite side of the option chain. The stop loss is the next larger reversal price above your put entry, or the next smaller one below your call entry.
- Stated another way: the stop-loss reversal sits two strike prices above whatever strike was resistance around 9:20, or two strikes below the 9:20 support strike. Resistance and support move during the day, so read them from the 9:20 snapshot.
- The target is the reversal price nearest to the current market price.
- A useful tell: if only the lower two 920 lines are drawn and the upper two are missing, expect a good bullish move that day. If only the upper two appear, expect a bearish one.

## What the video tells you to do

- Wait. Trading does not mean holding a position from 9:15 to 3:30. If neither the 920 lines nor the AI lines have been touched, there is no trade.
- Work through the filtered stock list one at a time and apply every rule; expect most to fail.
- Treat a target or stop that was reached within a rupee as reached.
- Never replace the tool's stop with a smaller one of your own choosing.
- Only trade F&O with genuinely surplus money, after term insurance and health insurance are in place. Never trade on borrowed money or a credit card.
