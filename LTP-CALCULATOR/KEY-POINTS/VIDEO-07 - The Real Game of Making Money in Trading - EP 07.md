## In one line

Grades a support or resistance level as strong, weak towards top or weak towards bottom, using the second-highest volume or open interest — shown in yellow in the LTP Calculator — as the challenger.

## Why grading the level matters

- Knowing where support and resistance are is only half the job. Whether the level actually turns the market depends on how strong it is.
- The comparison used: before climbing a branch you test whether it holds your weight.
- Levels are not fixed in place. Sometimes the market travels to the level; sometimes the level moves to the market. That movement comes from traders creating fresh demand and supply at particular strikes, and it is visible only on the Option Chain, not on a price chart.

## The three grades

1. **Strong** — the level has no meaningful challenger.
2. **Weak towards top (WTT)** — the challenger sits at a *higher* strike price than the level.
3. **Weak towards bottom (WTB)** — the challenger sits at a *lower* strike price than the level.

The speaker is explicit that "weak" here is a nickname rather than a verdict: a strong level can still fail to produce a reversal, which the Chart of Accuracy episode addresses.

## How to find the challenger

- The level itself is the highest volume or highest open interest. The challenger is the **second-highest** figure.
- The second-highest only counts once the LTP Calculator marks it yellow. No yellow means no impact.
- Yellow has five shades. The darker the yellow, the harder the second-highest is pressing on the highest, and the more it can change the decision.
- The rule is purely positional: yellow at a bigger strike than the level means weak towards top; yellow at a smaller strike means weak towards bottom. It applies the same way on the call side and the put side.

## Examples used

- Nifty resistance at 19,600 on volume of about 76 lakh, with the second-highest around 55 lakh and no yellow on it — strong resistance.
- Nifty support at 19,800 where the highest open interest does carry yellow — not strong.
- Bank Nifty resistance on the highest volume, but yellow — not strong.
- Godrej: resistance built on open interest alone with no yellow, so strong and unchallenged; support built on volume alone with yellow below it, so weak towards bottom.
- Hindustan Unilever: volume-only resistance with yellow at a lower strike — weak towards bottom.
- M&M Financial: highest volume at 930 with yellow at 940 — weak towards top.
- Another stock with the highest volume at 860 and yellow at 880 — weak towards top — and support on volume with yellow below it — weak towards bottom.

## What the video tells you to do

- For every level, answer four things in order: is it support or resistance; is it made of volume, open interest or both; at which strike price; and, if it is made of one factor only, is it strong, weak towards top or weak towards bottom.
- Use the home button in the LTP Calculator to open the list of stocks currently sitting at their support or resistance, and grade them one by one for practice.

## Worth knowing

- This episode only covers levels made of a single factor. Levels where volume and open interest coincide are handled in the next episode.
- The yellow shading is a feature of the speaker's paid tool, so the method as taught is not reproducible on a plain Option Chain without doing the second-highest comparison yourself.
