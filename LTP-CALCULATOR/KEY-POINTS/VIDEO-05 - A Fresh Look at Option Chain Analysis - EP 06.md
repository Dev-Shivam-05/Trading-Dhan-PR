## The definition of support

From the pair of strike prices on the imaginary line, on the **put side**, start at the **bigger** of the two strikes and move outwards towards the lower strikes. The first thing you meet — the highest open interest or the highest volume, whichever is closer to the imaginary line — is the support.

- This is the exact reverse of the resistance rule, which starts at the smaller strike and moves up the call side.
- Reason for the direction: the call side is pushing the imaginary line downwards, so the first strike below with enough weight behind it is what can stop the fall.
- As with resistance, support comes in three forms: volume only, open interest only, or both at the same strike.

## Worked examples

- **Nifty**: pair 19,550 and 19,600. Put open interest is about 1,39,000 at 19,600 and 3,21,000 at 19,500, but the largest volume — about 28 lakh at 19,550 — is met first. Support is 19,550, by volume.
- **Bank Nifty**: largest volume at the 44,300 strike, met before the largest open interest. Support is 44,300, by volume.
- **Adani Enterprises**: both support and resistance land at 2,500, both on volume.
- **AU Bank**: support at 700 on open interest; resistance at 720 on volume.
- **Dr Reddy's**: both the highest open interest and the highest volume sit above the imaginary line, and support is read at 5,600 on volume.
- One case is shown where only two strikes exist between the level and the line, which he sets aside for a later episode.

## Why volume-based levels are steadier

- Volume never falls during the session. Like an arrow already fired, a trade cannot be taken back — the figure only rises through the day, then resets to zero the next morning.
- Open interest can fall, because it represents writers' standing positions and a writer can buy back and close out.
- That is why an Option Chain shows a change column for open interest but never one for volume.
- Consequence: a support or resistance made only of open interest can shift quickly, so it carries more volatility risk. One made only of volume changes far less. A level where volume and open interest coincide is the most dependable of the three.
- The one thing that can weaken a volume level is the second-highest volume rising quickly alongside it. He mentions a percentage-growth comparison feature for tracking that, explained in the next episode.

## What the video tells you to do

- Read every level twice: first *where* it is, then *what it is made of* — volume, open interest, or both.
- Prefer levels built on volume, or on both, when deciding how much to rely on a reversal.
- Watch whether the level is shifting in a healthy direction: resistance moving to a higher strike and support to a lower one can still be relied on. Resistance moving lower or support moving higher cannot.
