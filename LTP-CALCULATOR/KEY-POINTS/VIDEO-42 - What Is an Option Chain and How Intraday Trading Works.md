# What Is an Option Chain and How Intraday Trading Works

## In one line

A beginner's tour of the option chain screen in the LTP Calculator — what each column means, where the imaginary line sits, and what Open Interest actually counts.

## Reading the option chain screen

- The strike price column runs down the middle in orange. Call data sits on the left, Put data on the right.
- Strike gaps are set by the exchange, not the trader. Nifty strikes are 50 points apart (21500, 21550, 21600). Bank Nifty strikes are 100 points apart (45800, 45900).
- Lot size is also set by the exchange and differs by script. It is shown at the far right of the screen. Nifty's lot size is given as 50.
- The blue box above the strike column shows the spot price — the market's current last traded price.
- Next to each strike, in order, sit LTP, change in points, volume, Open Interest and change in Open Interest. Open Interest is the fourth column on each side.

## The imaginary line

- A horizontal red line cuts across the chain. Above it on the Call side and below it on the Put side the background is shaded grey.
- The line always sits between the two strikes that bracket the current spot price. Those two strikes are called the **pair of strike prices at the imaginary line**.
- Example given: with Bank Nifty spot near 45,863, the pair is 45800 below and 45900 above.
- The line moves during the day, because it follows the market price.
- All calculations in the LTP Calculator's option chain are based on the **spot price**, not the futures price.

## Calls, Puts and writers

- A trader expecting the market to rise buys a Call; a trader expecting a fall buys a Put. The premium is a small outlay that moves in proportion to the underlying instead of buying the whole position.
- A writer is a seller who opens the position first and buys it back later. The example used is a gold dealer who sells today hoping to buy the stock back cheaper before settling.
- A trade is only complete once both a buy and a sell have happened, in either order.

## What Open Interest actually measures

- Only writers generate Open Interest. A buyer's purchase does not create it.
- Open Interest is the number of open positions that have actually been taken up — offered contracts that found a buyer.
- The quiz used to make the point: 25 writers offer 3,000 Nifty lots and buyers take 100 lots. Open Interest is **100**, not 3,000 and not 2,900.
- Analogy repeated through the video: a 50-seat classroom with 30 students present has an Open Interest of 30. Unsold seats, and traders who never entered, are irrelevant.
- With no buyer at all, Open Interest is zero, however large the offer.

## Where support and resistance come from

- Support is always found on the Put side (right of the strike column). Resistance is always found on the Call side (left).
- Both are built from a combination of Open Interest and volume. Volume is left for the next episode.

## What the video tells you to do

- Start at the option chain rather than at candlesticks or chart patterns — the guest says he learned the market this way and never studied candles.
- Before analysing anything, identify the script, its lot size, the spot price and the pair of strikes at the imaginary line.
- Read support only on the Put side and resistance only on the Call side.
