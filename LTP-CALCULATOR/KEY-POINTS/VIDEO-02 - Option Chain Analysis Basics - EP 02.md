Answers the question left at the end of Episode 1: the imaginary line moves through the day, and the pair of strike prices it sits between is the first thing to read off the Option Chain.

## How the imaginary line moves

- The line does not stay fixed. It shifts every time the market price crosses out of the strike band it is currently inside.
- Worked example from the video, starting with the market at 19,553 and the line between the 19,550 and 19,600 strikes:
  - Market rises past 19,600 — the line moves up to sit between 19,600 and 19,650.
  - Market rises 200 points to 19,753 — the line sits between 19,750 and 19,800.
  - Market falls to 19,353 — the line sits between 19,350 and 19,400.
- So the line simply tracks the two strikes that bracket the last traded price.

## The pair of strike prices

- The two strikes immediately above and below the line are given a name: the **pair of strike prices on the imaginary line**.
- In the opening example that pair is 19,550 and 19,600.
- This pair is the starting point the later episodes use to locate support and resistance, so reading it correctly matters.

## Top, bottom and direction

- In this system, the **top** of the Option Chain is the highest strike price and the **bottom** is the lowest strike price.
- A rising market is called **movement towards top** — the imaginary line travels towards the higher strikes.
- A falling market is called **movement towards bottom**.
- The speaker flags that this is not the same thing as "weak towards top", a separate term taught in a later episode. The two are easy to confuse.

## What the video tells you to do

- On each new market price, identify the pair of strike prices the imaginary line is now sitting between, and watch it shift as price moves.
- Label the direction as movement towards top or movement towards bottom rather than simply bullish or bearish, so the vocabulary matches the later episodes.
