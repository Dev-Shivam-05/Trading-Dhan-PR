## In one line

Episode two of Seven Days Challenge season three, explaining how volume and open interest shift between strikes to create bullish or bearish pressure, and how the nine scenarios of the speaker's Chart of Accuracy 1.0 turn that pressure into a verdict for the day.

## The three states a strike can be in

- Find the strike carrying 100% volume or 100% open interest, then look for any neighbouring strike holding 75% or more of that figure.
- If no neighbour reaches 75%, the level is strong - it is not moving anywhere.
- If the 75%-plus neighbour sits at a higher strike, the level is weak towards top. If it sits at a lower strike, weak towards bottom.
- The app highlights the strongest qualifying neighbour in yellow and leaves other 75%-plus strikes in bold. Read the yellow one, not whichever number catches your eye - the guest repeatedly picked 79% over 86% on screen.
- A highlighted strike that is deep in the money carries no meaning. Only levels found by scanning outward from the current price count.

## The exception that reverses the reading

- Weak towards top does not automatically mean bullish. If the level itself has already shifted down from a higher strike, a weak-towards-top reading is actually bearish pressure.
- The reverse holds too: a level that shifted up from a lower strike shows bullish pressure even while it reads weak towards bottom.
- The app prints "shifted from top to bottom" or "shifted from bottom to top" above the level. Either read that line or track the chain from 9:15 yourself.
- This is why the chart uses arrows rather than the labels WTT and WTB. The speaker says he is explaining the reason publicly for the first time: the same label can mean opposite things, so the arrow records the net pressure instead. An arrow running from a smaller strike to a larger one is bullish; the reverse is bearish.

## The nine scenarios

- Resistance pressure and support pressure combine into one verdict, which the app prints at the top of the screen.
- Both sides strong - neutral. Calls and puts are both available.
- Support strong, resistance bearish - slightly bearish, prefer puts. Support strong, resistance bullish - slightly bullish, prefer calls.
- Resistance strong, support bearish - slightly bearish. Resistance strong, support bullish - slightly bullish.
- Both sides bearish - blood bath, puts only. Both sides bullish - bull run, calls only.
- The last two scenarios cover support and resistance pulling apart or pressing directly on each other. Both are marked risky on both sides.
- Each pressure state can be reached three different ways - direct weak-towards, a shift, or a weak-towards that turned strong - which is where the claimed total of about 13,000 combinations comes from.
- Practical note from one example: when support and resistance are far apart and each edges towards the other without contesting the same strike, volatility stays low and the market consolidates.

## State of confusion, and the live example

- SOC appears in the middle four scenarios, where one side is strong and the other has been pushing for an hour or more without the market actually moving.
- After an hour it becomes SOC 1R, after two hours 2R, after three hours 3R. A bullish SOC means buy; a bearish SOC means sell. The market moves against the failed pressure.
- On the day of recording Nifty opened in blood bath with resistance weak towards bottom. Price refused to break the extension of support, and at about 10:15 the app flagged a bullish SOC.
- Price reversed up from the extension of support and rose until roughly 11:12, when the resistance shifted, the SOC ended at 100%, and the blood bath verdict returned. The market fell for the rest of the session.
- When an SOC ends, the pressure that was blocked takes over again, so that moment deserves close attention.

## What the video tells you to do

- Judge the direction of the pressure, not the label. Ask which way the level is being pushed at the moment you are about to trade.
- Cross-check the support and resistance values printed by the app against the strike you picked by eye before acting on either.
- Reach a conclusion about what the market is doing before taking any trade. Watching the chain without concluding anything is pointless.
- In a blood bath take only puts; in a bull run only calls; in the two risky scenarios stay out.
