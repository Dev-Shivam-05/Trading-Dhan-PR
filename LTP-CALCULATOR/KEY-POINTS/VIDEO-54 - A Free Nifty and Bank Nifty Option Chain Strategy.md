## The seven market-direction readings

The line above the chart names one of seven states, and everything else follows from it:

1. Neutral — no direction, price stays inside one or two ranges.
2. Slight bullish — prefer buying from the lower levels.
3. Bull run — a strong upward move expected.
4. Slight bearish — direction is downward.
5. Blood bath — a large fall.
6. Both sides risky, eighth scenario.
7. Both sides risky, ninth scenario.

The reading can change within the first minutes. The video's example flips from slight bearish to slight bullish about thirty seconds after the open.

## The eight AI lines

Red lines carry an R and relate to bearish trades; green lines carry an S and relate to bullish trades.

| Line | What it is for |
|---|---|
| R Max Pain | Stop loss for any bearish trade (dotted) |
| R Moderate | Conservative bearish entry, smallest stop |
| R Risky | Bearish entry for risk takers |
| R Max Gain | Exit and target for a bearish trade (dotted) |
| S Risky | Bullish entry for risk takers |
| S Moderate | Conservative bullish entry |
| S Max Pain | Stop loss for any bullish trade (dotted) |
| S Max Gain | Exit and target for a bullish trade (dotted) |

- Not all eight appear every day. Anywhere from two to eight can be drawn, and the number that appears is itself part of the signal. The AILTP button toggles them off, leaving a blank chart.
- The speaker's stated basis is a reversal-price model: when the demand-supply ratio shifts enough, price can reverse, and the strongest reversal price is picked using volume and open interest at each strike, with Option Greeks in the calculation.

## What the video tells you to do

- Wait five to seven minutes after the open before acting. Data fluctuates heavily in the first minutes and the reading can reverse.
- Read the direction line first, then pick the side of the chart you are allowed to trade.
- Use R Max Pain as the stop loss on any bearish trade and S Max Pain on any bullish trade, whatever method you actually used to enter, and exit at the matching Max Gain.
- If you are conservative, wait for the Moderate line; the Risky line is explicitly for traders willing to take a wider stop.
