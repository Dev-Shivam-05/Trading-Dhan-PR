## Indicator versus oscillator

- Every oscillator is an indicator, but not every indicator is an oscillator.
- An indicator's range may be open-ended; a moving average has no upper bound. An oscillator's range is fixed - RSI runs 0 to 100 with zones at 70 and 30.
- Indicators such as supertrend can identify a trend. An oscillator cannot, and was never built to. Its only job is to mark overbought and oversold zones.
- An indicator can work in both trending and range-bound markets. An oscillator is built for range-bound markets only.

## How RSI is actually calculated

- RSI = 100 minus 100 divided by (1 + RS), where RS is average gain divided by average loss. The 100 is fixed regardless of whether the stock trades at Rs 100 or Rs 26,000.
- The period setting counts candles, not days. Fourteen on a five-minute chart means the last fourteen five-minute candles - that is, the last seventy minutes. It means fourteen days only if you are on a daily chart.
- Influencers who select a five-minute chart and then describe "14-day RSI" are simply wrong about what the setting does.
- Worked example on a Rs 100 stock over fourteen candles: gains totalling Rs 17 and losses totalling Rs 7. Average gain 17/14 = 1.21, average loss 7/14 = 0.5, so RS = 2.42. That gives 100 minus 100/3.42, an RSI of roughly 70.8.
- Reading the formula backwards: the more gains outweigh losses, the larger RS becomes and the closer RSI is driven towards 100.

## The three conditions where RSI works

- A range-bound market. Gains and losses come out roughly equal, so RSI naturally oscillates inside the 30-70 band and the extremes mean something.
- A weak trend. Price grinds up or down slowly, RSI repeatedly reaches an extreme and stalls there, giving an early warning.
- A market rotating between two levels with momentum lost - the case the speaker maps to his own D1/D2 divergence rule.

## The three conditions where it fails

- A strong trend. A run of gains pushes RSI above 70, then 80, then 90, where it sticks because the scale cannot exceed 100. Selling the overbought reading while price keeps rising is how the money goes.
- News or a spike. A sudden burst of gains in ten of the last fourteen candles makes RSI overreact, print overbought immediately and stay there.
- A very low time frame. Fourteen one-minute candles on a stock that traded only a handful of times produce a value built on almost no data, so the reading is effectively random. An index holds up better than a single stock here.

## What the video tells you to do

- Establish the market's trend state before deciding whether to use RSI at all, and drop the indicator when the market is trending.
- Do not treat 70 as a sell instruction or 30 as a buy instruction. They mark zones, not trades.
- Match the period setting to the candle you are on and know how many minutes of data it actually covers.
