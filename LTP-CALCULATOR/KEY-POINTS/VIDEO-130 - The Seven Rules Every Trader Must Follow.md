## Spot, futures and options — the practical differences

- Three prices exist for a tradable stock: spot, futures and option. All three can be traded.
- In spot you can buy a single share. In futures you must buy the whole lot — the example stock quoted a lot size of 1,250 shares at a spot price of ₹444.
- A spot purchase can be held indefinitely. A futures contract expires on the last Tuesday of the month and must be closed on that day, then reopened in the next contract.
- A short sale in spot must be bought back the same day. A short in futures can be carried until expiry. That carry facility is the reason futures exist.
- Nifty has no spot instrument. A Nifty level can only be traded through its futures or its options.
- Buying means buying a Call. Selling means buying a Put. (Writing calls and puts is a separate topic, left for later episodes.)
- Spot and futures trade at different prices. If a level is quoted on the spot chart, the futures order will fill at the futures price for that moment, not at the quoted spot number.

## What a realistic return looks like

- A bank fixed deposit pays roughly 6–9%. Mutual funds average around 12.5% over a ten-year horizon.
- 25–30% a year from the market is described as a large result, not a starting point. Good traders are quoted as being content with about 1.5–2% a month.
- Doubling money in five days is dismissed outright — on ₹1 lakh that would mean ₹5,000 a day, which the speaker says nobody has sustained for ten years.
- Expect to spend a decade in the market before the returns are reliable.

## Reading the market from big to small

- The speaker uses a political analogy: Prime Minister down to Chief Minister, MLA, councillor. That direction is macro to micro.
- Intraday trading needs the same direction, but there is no point starting from a ten-year view. Start at the week.
- Order of questions: where will the market move this week, then where will it move today, then where exactly do I enter.
- The LTP Calculator's buttons are laid out in that same sequence, which is why the speaker says it should be read in that order.

## What the video tells you to do

- Set your return expectation at 25–30% a year before you place a trade.
- Trade one lot only for the first year. Increase to two lots after your reactions have settled, then give that another year or so.
- Before entering any trade, ask how much you stand to lose, not how much you stand to make.
- Begin each day with the weekly range, then the day's range, then the entry. Do not start with the entry tool.
- Take no trade at all on a day you do not understand.
