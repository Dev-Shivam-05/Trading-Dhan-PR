A replay of 2 May showing that most of an intraday session offers no tradable setup, and how to use the stop-loss-to-target ratio on each AI line to decide which of them is worth taking.

## Reading the screen before anything else

- Check the market-state line at the top first: bull run, both sides risky, blood bath and so on. Then select AILTP from the three tabs on the right (the others are Live Lines and 920 Lines).
- The option chain sits on the left and the chart on the right, with a draggable divider.
- A speaker button and a chat button in the top corner announce every change of market state, by voice or in writing.

## What the lines do and do not show

- Lines only appear for the side the current state permits. In a bull run no red resistance lines are drawn at all, because bearish trades are off the table. In a blood bath the S lines turn grey and buying disappears.
- A grey line means that trade has vanished; it was there before but is no longer valid.
- The market state changed repeatedly in the first minutes: bull run, then both sides risky, then blood bath, then back again. The advice is to wait three to five minutes and let it settle.
- Both sides risky means trades exist on both sides but each carries risk; blood bath means selling from the top only.

## The ratio test that drives the decision

- Whatever line you enter from, Max Pain is the stop loss and Max Gain is the target. R lines use R Max Pain and R Max Gain; S lines use the S pair.
- Before entering, measure both. In the replay R Risky offered a 200-point stop for a 100-point target, so it was rejected. R Moderate offered a 100-point stop for a much larger target, so it was the one worth waiting for.
- Price first reached R Moderate around 10:10 to 10:15 — roughly an hour after the open. Everything before that was a wait, not a missed trade.
- Every reversal price between your entry and Max Gain is a divergence, and each one is a valid place to book part of the position. From an entry near 580 the first divergence was 535 and the second, which was also R Max Gain, was around 490.
- If price touches the same line a second or third time, the risk on that touch is higher than on the first.
- If you enter at S Risky and price falls to S Moderate, the instruction is to average — add the same number of lots — rather than exit in fear.

## What the video tells you to do

- Wait three to five minutes after the open before trusting the state reading.
- Never take a trade whose stop loss is larger than its target. Compare the Risky and Moderate versions of the same side and take the better ratio, or take neither.
- Accept doing nothing. The tool is explicitly not designed to hand you a trade every session; on this day the only setup came an hour in.
- Book at whichever divergence suits you rather than always holding to Max Gain.
- If you already trade by price action, overlay the Max Pain and Max Gain lines on your existing method instead of abandoning it.
