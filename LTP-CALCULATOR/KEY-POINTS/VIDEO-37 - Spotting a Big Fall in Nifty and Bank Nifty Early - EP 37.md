## The signature of a big fall

- Ordinary intraday trades run from one divergence to the next. A blood bath is the exception: a single large move where the usual divergence-to-divergence target does not apply.
- The setup is scenario six of the Chart of Accuracy: **both** resistance and support are weak towards bottom. Not scenario one through five - only six.
- When this appears, buying calls is off the table entirely.
- A second, disguised version appears later in the day: resistance reading as strong but only because it has shifted down from a higher strike, with support still weak towards bottom. It looks like scenario four to the naked eye and is actually scenario six.

## Finding the entry and the stopping point

- For the entry, click the call side of the nearest resistance strike to read its value. At the 21,950 strike this returned about 21,979-21,980, which is the aggressive place to join the fall.
- The safer version is to wait for the next divergence above that level instead. Which one you use depends on how much risk you accept.
- For the first target, use the rule the speaker states as "weak towards bottom plus one": take the put-side divergence of the weak strike. With the level at 21,900-21,950 this gave about 21,932, and that is where the first leg stopped.
- On the second leg, the nearest divergences read 21,936 and 21,985, with 21,849 available as a profit-booking level.
- In a genuine blood bath there is no reliable floor. Price may run through one, two, three or four divergences, so book progressively rather than predicting the bottom.
