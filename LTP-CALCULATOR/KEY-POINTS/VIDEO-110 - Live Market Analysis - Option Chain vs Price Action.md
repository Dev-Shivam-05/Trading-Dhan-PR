## The six lines the app draws

- S Risky comes from the strike where support is weak-towards-top; S Moderate from the support strike itself; S Max Pain from two strikes below support. Below S Max Pain, close up for the day - no stop loss belongs beyond it.
- The profit target line only appears once price actually reaches the entry level, so an empty target field simply means price has not arrived yet.

## Chart of Accuracy 2.0 and where it does not work

- Open the OI-change graph on a strike and you get two lines: green for the call side, red for the put side. Green on top with red falling is bearish; red rising above green is bullish.
- At 26,050 the two lines ran parallel for most of the morning, which is why price kept hitting that level and reversing rather than breaking out.
- Crucially, 2.0 is useless at the support or resistance strike itself. There the call and put open interest differ by an order of magnitude, and a few thousand new contracts cannot move a position that large - the speaker's image is a two-foot child trying to push over a six-foot man.
- Use it only at intermediate strikes where call and put open interest are roughly comparable. Later in the day 26,150 became comparable, and only then was it worth reading.

## What the video tells you to do

- Decide the market's direction first, then decide where to buy. Never pick a price first and rationalise a direction for it.
- Do not take a trade against the Chart of Accuracy 1.0 verdict, even when a reversal looks certain. On a "slightly bullish" day, no bearish trade - accept two trades a month if that is what it comes to.
- Do not buy the moment a bull run appears. Wait for price to fall back to S Risky or S Moderate, which on that day meant waiting roughly 100 and 180 points below the current price.
- Read a red candle in a bull run as the market travelling towards your entry, not as a signal that you missed the move.
- If price runs away before reaching your level, let it go. Buying late puts your stop loss hundreds of points behind.
- Stop trading a consolidating market. When neither side is winning, option buying earns nothing.
