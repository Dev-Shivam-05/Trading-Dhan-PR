## Why charts alone are the problem

- Chart-based positional traders repeatedly get caught by fake breakouts: price clears a level, the trader takes a position, and price comes straight back.
- With around 200 stocks in the F&O list, manually researching each chart is not practical.
- The proposed alternative is to start from Open Interest, which shows where the pressure sits before the chart confirms it.

## The swing scanner

- On the LTP Calculator home screen there is a swing tab (spoken as "IDD Swing") that lists only the stocks currently sitting close to their support or resistance - the ones worth researching for a positional trade.
- The list separates bullish and bearish candidates. On the recording day, 2 January 2024, the bullish side was completely empty and only bearish names appeared: Asian Paints, Delta Corp, GNFC, HDFC Life, Lupin, MRF, Naukri, Vedanta, Gujarat Gas, JK Cement, Marico, National Aluminium, Tata Consumer and Voltas.
- The speaker reads an empty bullish list as market-wide selling pressure. If no stock can turn bullish, the index cannot either - and Nifty printed a large red day candle that session.
- An appearance on the list is a research trigger, not a trade signal. Each name still has to be checked individually.

## Working the Asian Paints example

- At the 3,400 strike, call-side Open Interest read 145 and was flagged "strong". Clicking the call-side volume returned about 3,392 as the top value for the stock.
- The speaker then replays the LTP Calculator's historical data day by day to show how that ceiling formed:
  - 21 December: highest Open Interest at 3,300, strong on the put side and weak towards the top on the call side, with price near 3,291. That combination allowed a run up to 3,400 but blocked anything above it.
  - 27-28 December (expiry): highest Open Interest at 3,400, still weak towards the top, with fresh Open Interest starting to build at 3,500. Expiry-day positions unwind, so this reading is unreliable.
  - 29 December: price pinned at 3,400 with a build-up at 3,500 pushing it slightly above the level.
  - 1 January: the 3,400 Open Interest turned strong. That is the point the setup becomes a sell - the extension of resistance was around 3,400.
  - 2 January: price fell from 3,400 towards 3,300, made its low near 3,300 and retraced. By 3:30 pm there was still strong Open Interest at 3,400 with 3,300 acting as the target zone.
- The trade stays valid as long as price cannot get back above 3,400.

## What the video tells you to do

- Open the swing tab first and treat it as a filter, then research only the names it returns.
- Check whether the key strike's Open Interest is marked strong or weak. Strong is what turns a level into a tradeable ceiling or floor.
- Use the historical data feature to replay earlier days and see when and how the level was built, instead of judging it from one snapshot.
- Ignore or discount expiry-day Open Interest, since positions are being closed out that day.
- Take the short after the level turns strong, with the next Open Interest cluster below as the target.
