## In one line

What to do when support and resistance give opposite signals at the same strike: read the two "weakness" percentages against each other, and let the stronger one tell you which way the stuck market will break.

## The stuck scenario

- The example is Bank Nifty on 25 October, taken from about 9:19 am. Both support and resistance sat at 43,300, with the imaginary line between 43,300 and 43,400.
- Resistance was weak towards top, which argues bullish. Support was weak towards bottom, which argues bearish. The two cancel each other out.
- The speaker calls this scenario 8 and 9 of his Chart of Accuracy and says a normal option chain, a candlestick pattern or any indicator will simply lose money here, because the market gives no one-way path.
- The first thing such a market does is consolidate between the divergence of the Call side of 43,300 (about 43,350) and the divergence of the Put side (about 43,250) — a 100-point band. Inside that band you can sell at the top and buy at the bottom.

## Reading the percentage

- Next to each weak level the app shows a percentage, derived from the volume at the two strike prices being compared. It measures how hard that side is pulling.
- Whichever percentage is higher wins. On this day weakness towards bottom read 96–99 against 84–87 on top, so the pull was downward and the market was expected to overshoot below the extension of support.
- Two cautions on reading it. When the imaginary line moves, a level can turn grey and a "weak towards top" label can appear that is only an artefact — check the raw volume numbers, not the colour. And use the Compare feature to pick the right strike to compare against, or the percentage means nothing.
- A live example: 1 crore 35 lakh against 1 crore 11 lakh gave a weakness reading of 82.

## How the session actually played out

- Morning: resistance percentage stable, support percentage increasing — bearish. Then both went stable and the market held its 100-point band, giving repeatable buys near 43,250 and sells near 43,350–43,390.
- By 12:02 support weakness read 95 against resistance at 85, with volumes of about 63 lakh and 60 lakh at the two strikes. That is the setup for a real break, not just a range.
- The speaker then watched the volume gap at the support strikes close tick by tick: 96,000, then 70,000, then 40,000, then 7,000 — and the shift completed.
- Once support shifted, the target became the divergence of 42,900, roughly 42,890–42,907. The market went there.
- Entries offered: the divergence of 43,100 at about 43,188 as the high-risk sell, and the divergence of 43,200 at about 43,288 as the safer one.

## What the video tells you to do

- In a resistance-weak-top plus support-weak-bottom market, do not take a directional trade on the chart. Either trade the consolidation band or wait for the percentages to separate.
- Compare the two percentages continuously through the day and re-classify the market against the nine combinations rather than fixing a view in the morning.
- Watch the volume difference between the old and new support strikes. When it closes to near zero the shift is done and the next divergence becomes the target.
- Book profit at the divergence of 43,000 unless the resistance has turned strong by the time price gets there. Only if the resistance percentage starts decreasing do you hold for one more divergence down to 42,900.
