## In one line

What to do when a trade taken at a strong support or resistance goes against you: where the outer boundary of the move actually is, where to average, where the stop loss belongs, and why a losing bought option must never be held.

## The setup that traps people

- Case study: 2 November 2023, read from about 9:50 am. Both support and resistance were strong in themselves, around the 19,800 area.
- The extension of resistance sat near 19,820 and the extension of support near 19,760, and the market rotated between the two.
- The first touch of each extension is treated as a safe trade. On this day the extension of support was hit once around 9:15 and the extension of resistance once around 10 am.
- The danger is the second touch. A trader who arrives late, or misses that the first touch already happened, buys a Call at the extension of support — and the market breaks straight through it instead.

## The outer boundary

- When both support and resistance are strong, the speaker's claim is that the market cannot travel beyond what he calls the extra divergence on either side.
- Upper limit: click the Call side of the 19,850 strike, which gave 19,863.
- Lower limit: click the Put side of the 19,750 strike, which gave 19,720.
- So a Call position caught in the fall has a defined place to average — 19,720 — rather than an open-ended loss.

## What the video tells you to do

- Before taking a trade at an extension, check whether that extension has already been hit once earlier in the session. If it has, the next touch is not a safe entry.
- If a Call bought at the extension of support goes against you, average at the lower extra divergence (here 19,720) and wait for the market to come back.
- Place the stop loss below that strike price — in this example below 19,750 and then below 19,700. If price goes there, the scenario itself has changed.
- Average only if you trade multiple lots, adding a quantity matching what you already hold. A single-lot trader should not average; hold the position back to cost instead.
- Never hold a stuck bought option position in the hope it recovers. If you cannot exit cleanly, book the loss and get out.
