## The eight lines and what each one is for

- Switching on AI LTP draws up to eight labelled lines on the chart, four on the support side and four on the resistance side: S Max Gain, S Risky, S Moderate, S Max Pain and R Max Pain, R Risky, R Moderate, R Max Gain.
- The Max Pain lines are always the outermost pair. The Max Gain lines sit closest to the centre.
- Roles are fixed: the Risky or Moderate line is the entry, the Max Pain line on that side is the stop loss, and the Max Gain line is the target.
- A Moderate line gives a tighter stop loss than a Risky line because it sits nearer to Max Pain; a Risky line offers more movement but more risk.
- The tool also prints a scenario name at the top of the chart, for example "Nifty both side risky COA 8".

## Which scenarios allow which trades

- **Slight bullish** — all four support lines appear, but the resistance side shows only three (no R Moderate). Bullish trades are in favour of Chart of Accuracy 1.0; any bearish trade here is highly risky.
- **Slight bearish** — the mirror image. All four resistance lines appear, S Moderate is missing, and bearish trades are the favoured ones.
- **Bull run** — only the four support lines are shown. Bullish trades are in favour. A sell can only be attempted from S Max Gain, and only if resistance is strong.
- **Blood bath** — only the four resistance lines are shown. Bearish trades are in favour; a counter-trade from R Max Gain requires support to be strong.
- **Both side risky 8** — all eight lines show, but both directions are against COA 1.0.
- **Both side risky 9** — the same, minus R Moderate and S Moderate. This is the riskiest of the set; the guest says he now avoids trading it entirely.
- **Bullish SOC** (1, 2 or 3 hours) is traded exactly like a bull run. **Bearish SOC** is traded exactly like a blood bath.

## Reading strength: WTT, WTB and strong

- If volume or open interest is shifting towards a higher strike, the LTP Calculator highlights it yellow and labels it WTT — weak towards top.
- Shifting towards a lower strike is labelled WTB — weak towards bottom.
- No label means strong: the level is not trying to move in either direction.
- A counter-trend trade is only allowed when the level you are fading shows no yellow. In the live example the speaker refuses a sell at S Max Gain because resistance is WTT and its percentage is still rising; resistance then shifts and S Max Gain moves away, confirming the refusal.
