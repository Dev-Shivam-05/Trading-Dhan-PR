## Open interest and volume are only completed trades

- Volume is created the moment a trade executes. Open interest is created when a sold, or written, position is executed.
- Bid and ask quantities are not trades. A bid at ₹8.30 and an ask at ₹9.25 with no match creates nothing — no LTP, no volume, no open interest.
- The worked example: the speaker offers five lots for sale and the participant buys one. Open interest is one, not five and not six. Only what actually traded counts.
- The analogy used: two people sitting in a room can say they are buying the Burj Khalifa or contesting an election against the Prime Minister. Saying it changes nothing. Large bid and ask numbers on a screen are the same kind of talk.
- Both volume and open interest are created by ordinary retail traders. That is why they are worth reading.

## Expiry and squaring off

- Cash never expires. A short sale in the cash segment must be bought back the same day.
- Futures expire on the last Tuesday of the month, and a futures position — long or short — must be squared off then.
- Nifty options expire every Tuesday; the new position is built on Wednesday. Bank Nifty, Fin Nifty, Midcap Nifty and stock options are squared off on the last Tuesday of the month.
- In futures and options a position can be sold before it is bought and held until expiry.

## Market orders versus limit orders

- A market order fills at whatever price the standing ask offers, not at the last traded price.
- Nifty example: LTP was 39.85 but the market order would fill at 40.70, about a rupee worse, because that was the resting ask.
- A stock option example is worse. An Ambuja Cements put showed an LTP of ₹14.70, while the market order filled at ₹16.55 — roughly ₹2 higher, on a trade whose whole target was about ₹2.
- The comparison used is buying potatoes at whatever price the seller names without bargaining, or handing over a blank cheque. Had the resting ask been ₹50, the market order would have paid ₹50.
- The instruction is firm: use limit price for entry, for the target and for the stop loss.

## How support and resistance are read from the chain

- The imaginary line is the speaker's own term for plotting the spot price between the two strike prices that bracket it. With spot at 890, the line sits between the 850 and 900 strikes.
- Resistance: start from the lower strike of that pair on the call side, move outward toward out-of-the-money, and take whichever of the highest open interest or the highest volume sits closest to the imaginary line.
- Support: start from the upper strike of the pair on the put side, move outward, and apply the same rule.
- Example given: highest open interest at 25000, highest volume at 25100. The 25000 strike is nearer the line, so that day's resistance is an open-interest resistance at 25000.
- The chain's AI now prints support and resistance at the top of the screen. Once you have the strike, look at that row and see which column reads 100% — that tells you whether the level is built on volume, on open interest, or on both.
- The repeated correction to the participant: look at the labels above the chain instead of scanning up and down inside it. The speaker drills this through Nifty, Bank Nifty, Axis Bank, DLF, HDFC Bank and Alkem.
- Underlying principle stated throughout: "the market is never motiveless." It leaves one level with a destination in mind, the way a person leaves home for the shop. The shop is resistance, home is support, and an extra errand two kilometres further is an extension of that level. Absent a major news shock, price moves within a range and reverses at its edge.

## What the video tells you to do

- Judge activity by executed trades, not by the bid and ask quantities on display.
- Place entries, targets and stop losses as limit orders, never market orders — especially in stock options where the spread is wide.
- Read support and resistance off the top-of-screen labels, then confirm at that strike whether the 100% reading is volume, open interest or both.
- Note the expiry rules: cash never expires, futures on the last Tuesday of the month, Nifty options every Tuesday.
