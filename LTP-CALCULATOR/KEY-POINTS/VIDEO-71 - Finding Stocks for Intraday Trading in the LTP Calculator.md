## How the screening was done

- If neither has been touched, the stock is an **open trade** and can still be entered.

## The filters applied

- **Either level already touched means skip.** Tata Steel was rejected because both its target of 158.2 and its stop had been reached — the rule given was that if the target *or* the stop has been hit, the trade is skipped. Axis Bank was rejected because its 1,159.0 target was already met at 1,157.78.
- **Risk decides the side.** Piramal had bullish risk 0 but bearish risk 3, so only the C1 buy was allowed and P1 could never be traded that day. Sun Pharma was dropped outright for bearish risk 2.
- A stock that cannot be traded at C1 cannot be traded at P1 either, but may become tradable at C2 or P2 later. Tata Steel was kept on watch for a C2 entry.
- **Read the direction correctly.** On a red sell line the target is below the entry and the stop is above it. The student initially checked the wrong side on Bajaj Finserv and was corrected: imagine you are the seller, so a rise to 2,039.1 hurts you and a fall to 2,017.1 is the target.
- **Lower risk wins a tie.** With two bearish open trades available, ICICI Bank (risk 0) was preferred over Bajaj Finserv (risk 1).
- The market state is not used in this screen at all. The teacher noted explicitly that blood bath and similar scenarios play no part in the stock list.

## What the video tells you to do

- Do the screening on your own screen rather than watching someone else do it.
- For every stock, check both the target and the stop against the candles already formed, in that order, before calling it an open trade.
- Think of yourself as the seller when reading a red line, so you do not mix up which side is the loss.
- When two valid setups compete, take the one with the lower risk number.
- Re-check the whole list once you have finished, as the student was asked to do.
