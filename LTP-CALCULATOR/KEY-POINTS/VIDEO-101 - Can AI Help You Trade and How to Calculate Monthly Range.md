## Trading the monthly range

- In the stocks section of the tool, clicking **M** draws the monthly range: three lines above the price and three below.
- The nearest line is L1. The speaker attributes standard-deviation probabilities to them: roughly 65% at L1, 95% at L2, 99% at L3 that price reverses from there.
- Trade the extremes only. Sell futures or write calls when a stock is sitting at a resistance line; buy cash or futures at a support line.
- **Timing rule:** the position must be opened in the first 15 days of the month, which is about the first 11 of the 22 trading days. A stock that only reaches L1 on the 26th is left alone because too little of the month remains.
- If price runs past L1 to L2, the speaker's approach is to average into the position, since L2 carries the higher reversal probability.
- Worked examples: GAIL sold at L1 near 179 fell to 173, about 4%. ITC sold near 420 fell to 398, about 3%. Divi's Laboratories made a low of 5,903 against an L1 of 5,835, so the level was never touched and no trade existed.

- No trendlines, candlestick reading, historical study or indicators are used in this method.

## The LTP Swing screener

- Path: Reports → LTP Swing. The screen lists bullish and bearish candidates separately.
- For a long: select bullish, set shifting status to **Strong or WTT**, and take only names with a star rating of **0 or 1**. Anything higher is skipped.
- The screen prints the entry level and a target. Example given: Concor at 524.8 with a target of 564, about 40 points. Another name at 648 with entry at 638 and target 703.
- **The stop loss is not a price.** You exit when the open-interest shift flips — WTT becoming WTB kills a long, and Strong becoming WTT kills a short. You exit on that flip even if the position is currently in profit.
- For a short: set status to Strong or WTB and reverse the logic. ONGC example: sell at 240, target 234.
- The same 15-day rule applies: open these positions in the first half of the month.

## Cash-futures arbitrage

- Open in the first two or three days of the month: sell one futures lot and simultaneously buy the identical share quantity in cash.
- Example: Aditya Birla Capital with spot at 278, futures at 280 and a lot size of 3,100 — sell one lot of futures, buy 3,100 shares in cash.
- Close both legs when the spot and futures prices converge, which happens naturally as the month ends.
- The tool shows an expected profit figure per stock. Opened at the start of the month one example showed about ₹6,200; the same trade taken near month end showed about ₹78, because the gap had already closed.
- Index contracts are excluded — this is done in individual stocks only.
- The speaker calls it a no-loss strategy, but notes returns are low, brokerage eats into them, and the capital required is large.
