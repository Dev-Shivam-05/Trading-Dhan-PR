# The Game of Percentage in Volume and Open Interest

## In one line

How to read the rising or falling percentage on a shifting strike to judge how long the current momentum will last, and why the same percentage means the opposite thing once a level has finished shifting.

## What the percentage is

- Every strike near support or resistance carries a percentage figure. It measures how strongly volume or open interest is pulling that level towards a neighbouring strike.
- The direction the level is pulling is labelled WTB (weak towards bottom, pulling to a lower strike) or WTT (weak towards top, pulling to a higher strike).
- The interpretation depends on the label, not on the number alone:
  - Resistance in **WTB** — a rising percentage drags the market down, a falling percentage lets it rise.
  - Resistance in **WTT** — a rising percentage lifts the market, a falling percentage weakens it.
  - When **both** sides' percentages are falling, the reading is bullish.
- **The critical point:** once a level completes its shift and settles at the new strike, the reaction inverts. The same rising percentage that was bullish before the shift becomes bearish after it. This was the guest's main misunderstanding, and the speaker spends much of the episode correcting it.

## Watch all four numbers

- There are four figures to track — the pulling percentages on the call side and the put side, in both directions.
- The trap described: you watch one number falling and conclude bullish, while a lower strike on the same side is quietly climbing and will pull the market down. The tool bolds these so they are not missed.
- When percentages on opposite sides both rise, a tug of war results. The market either goes nowhere or follows whichever side is stronger.

## How the percentage is actually used

- **It does not generate entries.** Entries still come from your value — a divergence, R Moderate, R Risky, S Risky.
- Its job is to tell you how much confidence to place in a trade before taking it, and how long to stay in it afterwards.
- Example from the replay: a bearish trade is taken at R Moderate on the expectation that the percentage will fall. Instead it climbs to 100% and holds there, so the position is exited even though it is flat.
- Another example: with a rising percentage fighting the setup, the speaker declines an R Risky sell until the number falls back to about 76, at which point he considers the trade acceptable.
- Red candles inside a bullish phase are not a reason to worry while the percentage is still falling. His phrasing: the candles are not people, they have no desires — they form because divergences have to be covered.

## Where the scenario overrules the percentage

- The scenario label still governs the direction. In a bearish SOC the rule remains to sell at R Risky regardless of what the percentages are doing.
- But the scenario itself has a lifespan. A bearish SOC exists because support or resistance is unsettled across two strikes. Once the level completes its shift and becomes strong at one strike, the scenario ends and the trade is no longer valid.
- So before taking the R Risky sell, check that the percentage has not run up towards 100 on the way there. If it has, the scenario is about to expire and the trade is skipped.
- When no percentage anywhere is pulling, the speaker says pure theory applies — the reversal levels work as published, with nothing distorting them.

## Setting the target

- The target is the **Max Gain** line: S Max Gain when trading up from support, R Max Gain when trading down from resistance. Hold until that line and exit there.
- If S Risky and S Max Gain print at the same place, there is no trade, even in a bull run — the entry and the target coincide.
- When the market runs above every line on the screen, it is outside the tool's tradeable range and nothing should be taken.
- Max Gain levels are recreated as the market moves. As resistance shifted higher through the session, new and higher Max Gain targets appeared behind it.
- Two workable approaches: wait for the Max Gain line, or set your own fixed point target and stop loss.

## What the video tells you to do

- Read the WTB or WTT label first, then the percentage, then decide what the percentage means.
- Re-read the label after any completed shift, because the meaning of the number reverses.
- Track all four percentage figures, not just the one that caught your eye.
- Take entries from your levels, and use the percentage only to size your confidence and to hold or exit.
- Exit a position when the percentage that justified it moves the wrong way, even at breakeven.
- Ignore two or four contrary candles while the percentage still supports the position.
- Check that a scenario is not about to expire before entering on its rule.
- Exit at the Max Gain line, and skip any setup where entry and Max Gain coincide.
